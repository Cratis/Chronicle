# A change to an observer's event-type set quiesces the observer through the Cratis.Orleans drain and owner admission, and is enabled per event store only by an operator's initialization on a single-version cluster with no observer jobs from before it

Proposed for Cratis/Chronicle#4955 and Cratis/Chronicle#4987 as the mechanism [decision 0010](0010-observer-event-types-are-the-union-of-registered-definitions.md) uses to apply a change to an observer's members; the two are proposed together and neither is in force without the other. It rests on two Cratis.Orleans guarantees: the drain barrier `StopAndDrain` (v1.20.0, which Chronicle runs) and owner admission (Cratis/Orleans#81, stage 2b, released in v1.21.0). Open points are under [Open questions](#open-questions).

Paths are relative to `Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/` unless they start with `Source/`, `Integration/` or `Documentation/`; paths marked *Orleans* are in Cratis/Orleans at `v1.20.0`, and those marked *Orleans 1.21* at `v1.21.0`; `Documentation/concepts/jobs.md` there is cited as *jobs.md*. Chronicle line numbers are at `bec5709f8c` (release 19.77.2).

## Context

A reader that runs across a membership change cannot be proven harmless against this code. The event sequence grain advances its in-memory tail after the storage commit (`Source/Kernel/Core/EventSequences/EventSequence.cs:206, 827-831`), so an old reader can pass an event the join position is computed below; old callbacks clear catch-up markers and transition state, not just positions; resumed jobs keep their old type set (`JobsManagerExtensions.cs:124-152`). Decision 0010 therefore needs the reader removed before a change, not reasoned about. The code it has to be removed from:

- **Stopping is not stopped.** Orleans persists `Stopped` before the steps stop. `StopAndDrain` (jobs.md, "Stop a job and wait until it is drained") is the acknowledged barrier, but it does not cover calls nobody awaits, such as the fire-and-forget `CaughtUp` (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:60-72`), and it does not keep a succeeded step's record: persisting a success deletes the step's state, and after a crash `OnStepCompletedOrStopped` can run twice or not at all.
- **Successful jobs disappear.** A job that completes successfully clears its stored state unless it keeps it (`KeepAfterCompleted`, false by default, *Orleans 1.21* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/Job.cs:62, 621-630`); neither the catch-up nor the replay job keeps it. A job's id is therefore not a durable record that it ran.
- **Admission precedes the first write.** `Start` asks admission and only then writes the job (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:157, 175`). Until that write lands, job storage does not know the job, and `IJobs.StopAndDrain` answers `NotFound` from storage without reaching the grain (*Orleans 1.21* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/Jobs.cs:94-99`). A `Start` for a stored execution answers `AlreadyBeenPrepared` (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:147-150`).
- **The observer interleaves.** The grain is not reentrant, but about twenty `IObserver` methods are `[AlwaysInterleave]` (`IObserver.cs`), among them the three `CaughtUp` overloads (`:456-476`), `RegisterCatchingUpPartitions` (`:447`) and `FailedPartitionRecovered`, `FailedPartitionNotRecovered` and `FailedPartitionPartiallyRecovered` (`:343-374`). They run at any await of any turn: a `CaughtUp` clears the catch-up markers (`Observer.Catchup.cs:170`), awaits its state write (`:181`) and then enters `Routing` (`:186-189`).
- **Checkpoints are debounced and steps are many.** A step's checkpoint becomes durable every `StepCheckpointBatchInterval` batches, 100 by default (`Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/Jobs.cs:51`), except for collapsing projections (`Jobs/HandleEventsForPartition.cs:69`); a reactor catch-up has one step per partition (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:115-172`), and a key can have a recovery job per failed partition.
- **Orleans 1.20 does not ask the owner on every path.** It resumes a job left `Running` or `StartingSteps` without calling `CanResume` (*Orleans* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/Job.cs:176-226`, `:396`), and advances stages without asking.
- **Unsubscribing pauses jobs.** `Unsubscribe` stops every non-replay job of the key (`Observer.cs:455-461`, `PauseJobs` `:1034-1048`), and only `ResumeJobs` (`:1155-1181`) resumes them.
- **Jobs started before this decision carry no revision,** and steps that succeeded under them have left nothing but their contribution to their job's aggregate handover (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:52-72`); Orleans rebuilds a job's step count from the steps that survive (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.Steps.cs:217-234`). What such a job covered cannot be reconstructed once it is stopped, so this decision never runs alongside one.
- **Every labeled merge to `main` releases,** so a multi-slice change ships as several releases.

## Decision

A change that adds a type to, or retires a type from, a namespace observer's members (decision 0010) runs as a **quiesce**: the observer stops admitting work, drains every job of its key through the job grain itself (each job is registered in a durable job inventory before its `Start` is sent), harvests positions from Chronicle's own progress and terminal records into per-partition rows, reconciles every unfinished obligation of the previous revision into exactly one successor, reads the committed tail from storage as the join position, and commits the next revision; only then does work resume. Every call from a job carries the revision it was created under and cannot move observer state across the change. Owner admission in Cratis.Orleans 1.21 keeps every job of an older revision, and every job that has already ended, from running again.

The quiesce, and decision 0010 with it, never coexist with state from before them. Upgrading the kernel changes nothing and blocks nothing. They are enabled for a running event store only by the operator's **initialize observers**, which refuses, observer by observer and with the reason, while any silo runs an older release or any observer job started before initialization exists; an event store created on a cluster that can run them is initialized at creation. Each namespace observer then takes its starting membership and contract from its own current registration, in its own turn.

### 1. Entering a change

- **Triggers:** a registration that declares a type that is not a member, and a retirement that passes its checks (decision 0010, sections 3 and 6). Initialization is not a change (section 9).
- **Latch first.** In the turn that starts the change, before its first await, the observer sets an in-memory latch and its running state to `ChangingDefinition`. It then writes durably the pending change, the running state and the committed tail `J0` (section 4). Its job inventory (section 2) is already durable; the change's enumerations add to it.
- **Stop admitting work.** The queue subscription is removed (leaving `Observing`), and `Handle` already drops batches while the observer is not `Active` (`Observer.Handling.cs:394`). Catch-up, partition recovery, retry reminders and replay requests are refused, and admission refuses the key's jobs (section 6). The watchdog only alerts: no unsubscribe on disconnect (`Observer.Watchdog.cs:170-212`; decision 0010's target removal still applies), no job rescue (`CheckJobTasks`, `:217-262`), no fast-forward (`:343-369`).
- **Quarantine does not defer a change.** A quarantined observer runs the change the same way: its jobs are drained whether stopped or not, and replays, which `PauseJobs` leaves running, are drained too. After the commit it stays `Quarantined`, and resumes when the quarantine ends. Deferring the change until then would put every event of an added type appended meanwhile below its fence, for as long as the quarantine lasts.

### 2. The job inventory and draining

- **The job inventory** is a set of rows keyed by observer key and job id, each with the job type, its kind (catch-up, partition catch-up, retry, hold resume, replay), its revision, the continuation or restart-plan row it was started from, if any, and its state: *intended*, *started* or *terminal* with an outcome (section 3). Rows are written and read in pages, at most `Observers.DefinitionChangeRowsPerTick` (1,000 by default) per tick, like continuations; neither a turn nor a stored document grows with the number of jobs.
- **Row states only move forward.** Every write of a row's state is conditional on its stored state: *intended* becomes *started* only while it is still *intended*; *intended* or *started* becomes *terminal*; *terminal* is absorbing, and nothing but its deletion (section 3) removes it. A `Start` that answers after its job already completed inside it, as an empty replay does, therefore finds the row *terminal* and leaves it so.
- **Registered before `Start`.** A job's row is written as *intended*, in the observer's turn and before `Start` is dispatched, with the job id the observer chose (`IJobs.Start(key, jobId, request)`, *Orleans* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/IJobs.cs:34`). It becomes *started* when `Start` answers. A `Start` whose answer is lost or timed out leaves the row *intended*: it still counts in the quiesce until it is drained and reconciled (section 3).
- **Drained through the grain.** A non-interleaving grain timer ticks every `Observers.DefinitionChangePollInterval` (1 second by default) and calls `StopAndDrain(TimeSpan.Zero)` on the job grain itself, addressed by the row's job type and id (`IGrainFactory.GetGrain<TJob>(jobId, key)` as `IJobDrain`), for rows not yet drained, at most `Observers.DefinitionChangeCallsPerTick` (100 by default) per tick and round-robin across ticks. It does not use `IJobs.StopAndDrain`, whose `NotFound` comes from storage. Job grains are not reentrant and neither `IJob` nor `IJobDrain` interleaves, so a drain that reaches a grain while its `Start` is in its turn runs after that turn has written the job and fanned out its steps, and drains them; a drain that reaches the grain first finds no execution and answers `Drained` (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.Drain.cs:69-72`), and the `Start` that arrives afterwards asks admission, which refuses it because the intent is durable (section 6). The grain's own answer is the proof that nothing of the job runs; storage `NotFound` is not.
- `Drained` and `Completed` count as drained; `TimedOut` and an error are polled again on a later tick. The observer never awaits a drain with a positive timeout: step work and job hooks call the observer's non-interleaving `ReportHandledEvents` and `PartitionFailed`, so a waiting drain and a waiting step would block each other (jobs.md, "Do not wait for a drain from a grain your job calls back into").
- **Enumeration** is a check, not a source: the key's unfinished jobs are enumerated in pages when the change starts and once more when every row has drained. Every job of an initialized observer is in the inventory before its `Start`, so a job the enumeration finds without a row is a fault; it gets a row, is drained and reconciled like an unresolved start, and is logged.
- `Drained` means no step's work or activation callback runs any more, each step's outcome and last checkpoint is in step storage, and the job's hooks have run. A step does not pass its cancellation into the subscriber wait (`Jobs/HandleEventsForPartition.cs:435`, `ObserverSubscriberExtensions.cs:33-59`), so it finishes the wait it is in, with an answer or the subscriber timeout, before it stops; a drain never abandons a subscriber wait.

### 3. Progress records, terminal rows, the harvest and successor obligations

- **When a step writes a progress record.** A step of an initialized observer writes a progress record (observer key, job id, partition, position, outcome) when its checkpoint has become durable (the debounced write in `ReportNewSuccessfullyHandledEvent`, `Jobs/HandleEventsForPartition.cs:101`) and at its terminal outcome: success, stop, failure or hold. That is one write per durable checkpoint and one per outcome, not one per batch.
- **A succeeded step's position** is the one it reports with success. Its record is written before it reports; if that write fails, the step does not report success and closes with a partial result, as a refused checkpoint callback already does (`ReportFromWork`, `ClosedResult`, `:404-417`). `Drained` guarantees every outcome of a drained step was reported, so its terminal record exists.
- **Terminal rows.** A job of an initialized observer records its outcome in its inventory row, as the first thing in `OnAllStepsCompleted` and before `CaughtUp`, `Replayed` or a recovery callback is sent: the catch-up handover (last handled, last scanned, whether every step succeeded), the replay's outcome, or the recovery's result. Orleans calls that hook before it clears a completed job's state and keeps a job whose hook throws as `Failed` (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:612-630`), so a job never disappears without its terminal row or its stored state.
- **Consuming a terminal row, in this order:** (1) apply its outcome to observer state and write it; (2) remove or settle the obligation it is linked to — the restart plan or continuation it was started from — and write that; (3) delete the job's progress records; (4) delete the terminal row last. Until step 4 the row keeps admission refusing a `Start` for its job id (section 6), so a plan whose job ended can never start it again, whatever step a crash interrupts. Applying an outcome is idempotent (positions only move forward, fence clearing and failure recording are set operations), so recovery simply runs the sequence again from step 1. Every consumer follows it:
  - the completion call (`CaughtUp`, `Replayed`, the recovery callbacks), outside a change;
  - the watchdog, outside a change, for a terminal row whose call has not arrived within the Orleans response timeout;
  - the observer's own resume after a restart or commit, for a row whose plan or continuation it finds (section 4), including a job that completed inside its own `Start`;
  - the harvest during a change, where steps 1 and 2 become part of the commit (the outcome is applied to what the commit publishes, and the linked obligation is not carried), and steps 3 and 4 run after it.

  A terminal row of an older revision than the current one was consumed by the commit that harvested it; finding one means a crash came between that commit and its deletions, and it is deleted without being applied again. A completion call whose row is gone changes nothing.
- **The harvested position** of a partition is the later of its record and its step's checkpoint in step storage.
- **Every record of the key is harvested,** not only those of the recorded jobs, and so is every terminal row, so a job that completed just before the change, whose `CaughtUp` the change turns into a no-op (section 5), loses nothing.
- **A failed entry beats a continuation.** A partition with a failed-partition entry, a failure or a hold, gets no continuation: a step that handled up to 70 and failed at 71 resumes from its entry at 71, as `ProbeAndStartCatchupJob` already skips failed partitions (`Observer.Catchup.cs:281`). A terminal failure record becomes a failed entry at its position when the observer has none, so a `PartitionFailed` still on its way adds nothing. A recovery job's record moves its entry's resume position only when the owner matches (decision 0010, section 5).
- **What a record becomes.** Catch-up and partition catch-up records become partition **continuations**; retry and hold-resume records become the failed partition's resume position. Replays are not harvested; each becomes a **restart plan**: its scope (the whole observer or one partition), its bounds, its event types (a full replay takes the new member set; a partition replay keeps its requested types that are still members, and clears without a job when none remain) and a fresh job id.
- **Exactly one successor per obligation.** Every unfinished obligation of revision *r* — a continuation or restart plan, and the inventory row of any job started from it, whether *intended*, *started* or *terminal* — is reconciled, after its job has drained, into exactly one successor of revision *r + 1*, from the evidence in this order:
  1. **The row is *terminal*:** the terminal row is consumed as above; the outcome is the successor (a completed catch-up needs no continuation; a completed replay needs no plan; a failed replay is not restarted and clears no fence). The origin row is not carried.
  2. **Otherwise the job has stored state** (the drained grain wrote it, so its `Start` ran): its execution is harvested like any drained job. A catch-up or partition catch-up gives continuations at the later of its origin position and what it recorded, a retry or hold resume gives the entry's resume position, and a replay gives a new restart plan for its scope and bounds with a fresh job id. The origin row is consumed by it, not carried.
  3. **Otherwise** no `Start` ran: the drain answered `Drained` for a grain with no execution, and any `Start` arriving later is refused (section 6). The origin obligation is carried to *r + 1* as it is, reconciled with the new members, with a fresh job id.

  A successor never reuses the job id of an execution that may exist, so `AlreadyBeenPrepared` cannot arise; the old job is deleted after the commit. The **lost response** case: a restart plan of *r + 1* starts job `j1`, the job runs, its answer is lost, and the row stays *intended*; a later registration starts the change to *r + 2*, which drains `j1`. If `j1` completed, its terminal row settles the plan. If it holds state, it is harvested, and the plan's successor is a new plan of *r + 2* with job `j2`. Only if it never ran is the plan carried, again with `j2`. The plan of *r + 1* is removed in every case, so one plan exists afterwards, never two.
- **Rows, not one document.** Continuations, restart plans and the job inventory are rows keyed by observer, revision and partition, plan or job, written over successive ticks, at most `Observers.DefinitionChangeRowsPerTick` per tick, so neither a turn nor a stored document grows with the partition or job count. Readers take rows of the current revision. Rows of revision *r + 1* belong to the pending change and are kept while it is pending; rows of an older revision are removed only after the commit that reconciled them. A crash mid-harvest re-enters from the intent and rewrites rows by key.
- **Sweep.** Outside a change, the watchdog tick consumes terminal rows whose completion call is overdue, as above, and removes progress records whose job has neither a row nor stored state and that are older than the Orleans response timeout. It never runs during a change.

### 4. Commit and resume

- **The commit turn** runs on the timer once every row is written. Before its first await it advances the in-memory revision to *r + 1*, so a revision-*r* call that resumes at any later await finds a newer revision (section 5). It waits for any observer state write already in flight, so a write prepared under revision *r* cannot land after the commit's.
- **Join position.** It reads the committed tail from storage (`IEventSequenceStorage.GetTailSequenceNumber`, `Source/Kernel/Storage/EventSequences/IEventSequenceStorage.cs:220`) on the primary: MongoDB primary read preference with majority read concern, the SQL primary. Each added type's fence is `J = tail + 1`, or `EventSequenceNumber.First` when the sequence is empty (`Unavailable` survives addition, `Source/Kernel/Concepts/Events/EventSequenceNumber.cs:15-20, 97`).
- **One write** of the control record publishes revision *r + 1*, the members with their fences, the registration identities and retirement results, and the outcomes of the terminal rows the harvest consumed, and clears the pending change (decision 0010, section 2). Then the old jobs, the consumed terminal rows and harvested progress records, and the rows of revision *r* are deleted, terminal rows last.
- **Missed events are counted, not owed.** Events of an added type with sequence numbers from `J0` up to `J` were appended while the change ran and lie below the fence. When there are any, a warning alert gives their count and recommends a replay; no per-type debt is recorded.
- **Restart plans first.** For each plan, the observer writes its job's inventory row as *intended* (unless one exists) and starts the job with the plan's job id. A *terminal* row for that id means the replay already ended: the row is consumed (section 3), which settles the plan, and nothing is started. Admission refuses a `Start` for a job id with a terminal row (section 6), and the row is written in the job grain's turn before Orleans clears its state, so a crash anywhere in this sequence — after the replacement completed and Orleans deleted it, or after an empty replay completed inside its own `Start` — starts the job at most once. While a full-replay plan is unconsumed the observer starts no catch-up and subscribes no live delivery; a partition-replay plan keeps its partition out of catch-up.
- **Then the rest.** The queue is subscribed with the new set and revision; catch-up runs from `Next` and excludes partitions with continuations, which get partition catch-ups from their continuation (`Observer.Catchup.cs:313-321`), each registered in the inventory before its `Start`; retries run from their failed partitions; holds whose awaited type is now declared or retired resume. A continuation is settled when its job's terminal row is consumed. An observer that is quarantined stays so and resumes when the quarantine ends.

### 5. Calls from jobs carry the revision

- Job requests, live queue batches and every call from step work or a job hook to the observer carry the revision they were created under. Completion calls carry the job id and are applied only through the job's terminal row (section 3).
- **During a change** the job calls that advance positions or clear state are no-ops, logged: the three `CaughtUp` overloads, `RegisterCatchingUpPartitions`, `FailedPartitionRecovered`, `FailedPartitionPartiallyRecovered` and `FailedPartitionNotRecovered`. The harvest carries what they would have carried. `PartitionFailed` and `ReportHandledEvents`, which do not interleave, still apply; a transition they would trigger waits for the resume.
- **Interleaving callbacks re-check after every await.** If the latch has moved since the call entered, because a change began or the revision advanced, the callback stops without a further mutation or transition. What it did before its first await belonged to the revision that was current then. A `CaughtUp` that cleared markers before a change and resumes after its commit therefore never reaches `TransitionTo<Routing>()` and cannot clear the markers of revision *r + 1*'s partition catch-ups (`Observer.Catchup.cs:317`).
- **After the commit** a call or batch of an older revision is ignored entirely: it advances no position, clears no marker and triggers no transition.

### 6. Owner admission (Cratis.Orleans 1.21)

A drain stops a job; it does not decide whether the job may run again. Chronicle uses the admission API of Cratis/Orleans#81, stage 2b:

- `IJobAdmission.MayRun(JobAdmissionRequest(JobKey, JobId, JobType, IJobRequest), CancellationToken)` returns `Task<bool>` and is registered with `siloBuilder.AddJobAdmission<T>()`, a singleton added with `TryAddEnumerable`. A 1.21 silo without the registration admits everything.
- It is asked at `Start` (refusal: `StartJobError.NotAdmitted`, 7), at every `Resume` — explicit, rehydration of a job left running or starting, and recovery — (refusal: `CannotResumeJobError.NotAdmitted`, 3; `IJobsManager.Resume` returns false) and at every stage advance. A preparing job is not asked on `Resume`, which starts nothing there; its `Start` was asked.
- Refusing a `Running` or `StartingSteps` job makes `Resume` write `Stopped` with a pending drain and answer `NotAdmitted` at once; the drain completes in the job's own timer turn. If that drain times out, the job stays stopped with the drain pending until the owner's `StopAndDrain` or its next activation; nothing restarts it. A refused stage advance stops the job and runs `OnStopped`.
- A refusal is final for that attempt; recovery and rehydration go through `Resume`, which asks again, so a job stays refused for as long as the owner refuses.
- Throwing, being cancelled or exceeding the time limit (the 1.20 drain bound, about half the Orleans response timeout) fails the operation and changes nothing; it is never a refusal. Rehydration leaves the job `Running` in storage and asks on the next pass; a stage advance retries the check on a timer.
- Orleans has no per-step check, and none is needed: step work starts only from the fan-out of an admitted `Start`, `Resume` or stage start, and a restart after an earlier stop or a deferred start happens only inside an admitted execution that has not been stopped. A job type with `StartStepsInBackground` is asked in `Start`'s turn and fans out on the next timer tick; only `Stop`, `StopAndDrain` or `Remove` can run in between, and each makes that tick a no-op.
- `MayRun` never calls the job, its steps, or a non-interleaving method of a grain that may be awaiting that job's `Start` or `Resume`.

Chronicle's `MayRun` reads the control record and the job's inventory row from storage on the primary and calls no grain. It decides in this order:

1. **Out of scope, or not initialized: admit.** A job of an observer kind decision 0010 leaves unchanged, or of an observer that is `NotInitialized` or `AwaitingRegistration` (section 9), is admitted as today. This keeps every slice inert until an operator initializes the store, and keeps an observer whose initialization was refused on today's behavior.
2. **The observer is initializing: refuse** every job of the key (section 9).
3. **The job has a terminal row: refuse.** A job that has ended never begins another execution under the same id.
4. **The job has no revision: refuse.** After initialization no job from before it may run.
5. **Otherwise** admit when its revision is current and no change is pending and, for a recovery job, its owner matches the failed-partition entry.

**Steps of a job without a revision check at their start.** Step work of a job whose request has no revision reads the control record when it starts and stops without reading if the observer is initializing or initialized. It is Chronicle's own step code, not an Orleans check, and it covers one case only: a `Start` sent before initialization whose first write was still pending when initialization checked for jobs (section 9). Its job state is written before any step starts, so it cannot deliver; such a job is deleted on sight.

The observer never awaits a refusal's drain in its own turn: `ResumeJobs` and the revision filter in `JobsManagerExtensions.cs:124-152` skip jobs of other revisions, so a job that admission would refuse while running reaches `MayRun` only through rehydration, outside the observer. Those filters, `CanResume` overrides that apply the same predicate, and deleting old jobs on sight are defense in depth; on their own they leave the paths Orleans 1.20 does not route through `CanResume` unguarded.

### 7. Why no reader crosses a change

Every job of an initialized observer is in the inventory before its `Start` is sent, and no job from before initialization can run (section 9); a `Start` admitted under revision *r* whose first write stalls past both enumerations is drained through its grain after its turn, or is refused when it arrives after a drain that found nothing (section 2). Once a job answers `Drained`, none of its steps reads, delivers or checkpoints again, and admission keeps it and every other job of revision *r* from running. `J` is read only after every job of the key has drained, so every position a revision-*r* reader scanned or passed held an event already committed and lies below `J`; whatever it skipped of an added type is history below the join, which decision 0010 excludes. Reading the committed tail from storage on the primary is what makes this hold: an event committed but not yet in the grain's in-memory tail is below `J`, and a lagging secondary cannot put `J` below an event an old reader passed. Appends are numbered and committed serially by the event sequence grain (`Source/Kernel/Core/EventSequences/EventSequence.cs:793-831`), so an append in flight during the tail read gets a number at or above `J` and is delivered. Every reader after the commit runs with revision *r + 1*, the full member set and the fences, and section 5 keeps a late revision-*r* call from moving anything. That the harvest spans several ticks changes none of this: nothing it reads can change once the jobs are drained.

### 8. Bounded, observable, recoverable

- **Pause.** With jobs running, a drain settles within the subscriber timeout (`Observers.SubscriberTimeout`, 30 seconds by default, `Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/Observers.cs:34`) plus `JobsOptions.StepStopTimeout` (10 seconds) of being requested, and a later tick sees it; the inventory adds a tick per 100 drain calls and the harvest a tick per 1,000 rows. A subscriber timeout at or below zero waits indefinitely, and a drain can stay stuck, for example on a step whose silo cannot drain; then only forced completion ends the pause.
- **Visible.** `Observers.DefinitionChangeTimeout` (5 minutes by default) raises a warning alert when exceeded while polling continues. The running state, the pending change, its age and the jobs still draining appear in the Workbench and the CLI. Metrics `chronicle-observer-definition-changes-in-progress` (gauge) and `chronicle-observer-definition-change-duration` (histogram); entry and exit logs carry the revision.
- **Forced completion.** **Complete definition change** with `--force` deletes the remaining jobs without harvesting them and commits; their unharvested progress is delivered again, and an undrained step could deliver out of order until it settles, so it is the one path that weakens per-partition order. Their plans and continuations are carried as in case 3 of section 3. It uses the kernel's existing authorization, which admits any authenticated client including applications (`Source/Kernel/Core/Sequences/ReopenStreamScope.cs:26`); it requires a reason and appends an audit-only system event recording who forced it, when and why, as retirement does, and the Workbench shows that event. Restricting kernel administration operations to an operator role is Cratis/Chronicle#5015.
- **Crashes.** Before the commit the change re-enters from the durable intent; a job already asked to drain completes its drain when next activated and refuses `Resume` until then; inventory rows, progress records, terminal rows and successor rows survive. After the commit, jobs and progress records of older revisions are deleted on sight, restart plans run first, and terminal rows, deleted last, make every start at most once. No path needs a manual storage edit.
- **Abandoned subscriber calls** are outside the drain. The subscriber timeout abandons the kernel's wait, not the subscriber's work (`ObserverSubscriberExtensions.cs:26-58`, `Reactors/Clients/ReactorObserverSubscriber.cs:56-60`), so a drained observer can still have client work outstanding from a wait abandoned before or during the drain. That partition failed, and its retry after the commit can overlap the abandoned call exactly as a retry after a timeout does today. This decision neither creates nor widens that overlap; it is tracked in Cratis/Chronicle#5014.

### 9. Enablement: no coexistence with state from before

- **An upgrade alone changes nothing.** A silo of the gate release registers a marker grain type, carrying its release version as a grain property, and `AddJobAdmission<T>()` together in `AddChronicleToSilo` (`Source/Kernel/Grpc/Setup/ChronicleServerSiloBuilderExtensions.cs:48`), and a spec asserts that registering the marker registers admission. That release builds on Cratis.Orleans 1.21. Until an event store is initialized, every observer of it behaves exactly as today: the registration replaces the set, the #4987 hand-over applies, admission admits everything of it (section 6, rule 1), and nothing new is written or read. Upgrading never blocks: nothing waits for initialization, and retire type and complete definition change answer `NotInitialized`.
- **Initialize observers** (`cratis chronicle observers initialize [--event-store <name>]`, the Workbench's *Initialize observers* action, and a Core `[Command]`) enables decision 0010 and this record for one running event store. It is an authorized operator command with an audit-only system event, as forced completion. An orchestration grain keyed by event store persists its progress and each namespace observer's result, so it is idempotent: running it again never repeats completed work, answers `AlreadyInitialized` for initialized observers, and retries only refused ones.
- **Cluster check.** It first reads the Orleans cluster manifest with a complete view of active membership. If any active silo lacks the marker, or its manifest is missing, it refuses the whole event store and names each such silo by address with its release version, or *older than the gate release* when it has no marker. Chronicle has no other silo-capability mechanism (`Source/Kernel/Contracts/Clients/KernelCapabilities.cs` is client-facing).
- **Then the event store becomes initialized,** in one write of event-store-level state, and from then on new namespaces and new observers in it are initialized at creation (below). Each existing namespace observer in scope is then asked, in its own grain turn, to initialize, and answers one of:
  - `Initialized` — it had no unfinished job;
  - `AwaitingRegistration` — it had none and no current registration: it keeps today's behavior and initializes in the turn of its next registration, which checks again for unfinished jobs and, finding any, leaves it `NotInitialized` with that refusal;
  - `Refused` with every reason — the observer, namespace and kind; each unfinished job with its id, type, status and when it started; and what stopping each one means: catch-up or replay restarts from the observer's own recorded position, and for a reactor, webhook or event-store subscription the partitions that job already completed are delivered to its handlers again. Event-store subscriptions append to the target's inbox without an idempotency key (`EventStoreSubscriptions/EventStoreSubscriptionObserverSubscriber.cs:92-105`), so duplicates there are appended events.
- **Clearing a refusal.** The operator lets the listed jobs complete, which delivers nothing again, or stops and removes them with `--stop-jobs`. That flag stops replay-safe observers' jobs (projections, reducers and read models, whose redelivery rebuilds the same state), and stops a side-effecting observer's jobs only when it is named in `--accept-duplicates <observer>`; otherwise that observer stays `Refused` with the duplicate consequence as its reason. Stopping runs inside the observer: it enters `Initializing` (durable), stops admitting work exactly as a change does (section 1) and admission refuses every job of its key (section 6, rule 2); it drains those jobs through their grains as in section 2, removes them, clears its catch-up markers, keeps `Next`, its failed partitions and its replay state, and initializes. Its next routing restarts catch-up or the replay from that recorded position under the new rules. The audit event lists, per observer, the jobs stopped and the duplicates accepted. A crash while `Initializing` continues from the durable state on reactivation.
- **What an observer initializes from.** In the turn that initializes it, the observer writes revision 1 of its control record (decision 0010, section 2): the members are the event types of its current, applied registration in that namespace, with no fences; its compatibility contract is that registration's subscriber type and filters (decision 0010, section 4). The cluster runs one release, so nothing else can have written them, and the namespace's own registration is the only source: the shared event-store-level definition is never read. A failed partition written before initialization has no mode and no owner; it is read as live mode, the only mode today's retries run in (`Jobs/RetryFailedPartition.cs:118-126`), resuming at its last attempt, where today's retry starts, and gets its owner when the observer starts a recovery job for it. A hold is never from before initialization.
- **Serialized with definition changes.** Initialization of a namespace observer runs in the turn of the grain that holds its authoritative definition, or after that grain has frozen its contract, so no definition change can interleave with it (decision 0010, section 4): a client reactor's observer initializes in its own turn, where its registrations are checked and applied; a webhook's or event-store subscription's namespace observers initialize only after the orchestration has asked the webhook grain or the event-store subscriptions manager, in that grain's turn, to freeze the contract, and from the definition it froze.
- **A job from before initialization that slipped through** — a `Start` sent before initialization whose first write landed after the job check — cannot run: admission refuses it at every `Resume` and stage advance (section 6, rule 4), and its steps stop at their start; the observer deletes it on sight and its own catch-up covers what it would have.
- **Created on a capable cluster.** An event store created while every active silo carries the marker is initialized at creation, and so is every namespace created in an initialized event store; its observers initialize at their first registration, which is their baseline. None of them can have state from before.
- **Visible.** The Workbench's observer list and details and `cratis chronicle observers show` give each namespace observer's initialization state (`NotInitialized`, `AwaitingRegistration`, `Initializing`, `Initialized`) and, when refused, the reasons of the latest attempt; the event store shows whether it is initialized and which silos block it.
- **The downgrade floor** is the gate release. A silo of any release from the state slice on that finds an initialized control record or event store refuses to activate the observer and logs that the kernel is below the floor. Releases before the state slice cannot be changed: an older silo rewriting a failed partition drops hold fields, and the new enum members are unknown to it. Initialization refuses while such a silo is active, so this arises only by downgrading or adding an older silo after it, which is unsupported. Before initialization a downgrade is free.
- **Delivery.** Slices land on `main` dark: schema additions are additive and nullable, nothing new is written before initialization, and new paths stay unreachable until it. The order: (1) decision 0010's reader scopes, which keep the legacy reading of every request; (2) state and storage for both records, including the progress-record, row, inventory and terminal stores, the compatibility contract, the event-store and observer initialization state, the `ChangingDefinition`, `Initializing` and `WaitingForHandler` members, and the downgrade refusal; (3) contracts, released as a minor so Cratis/cli builds against published packages, with their operations answering `NotInitialized`; (4) decision 0010's routing, holds and retirement; (5) this record's engine on Cratis.Orleans 1.21, with admission registered and admitting everything of an uninitialized event store; (6) Workbench, the CLI release, the .NET client and the documentation below; (7) the enablement, last: the marker, initialize observers with its checks, refusals and job stopping, initialization at creation, and the serialization with definition changes. The routing and engine slices are safe on their own only because nothing reaches them before initialization.
- **Documentation.** `Documentation/migrations/` gains an operator section on upgrading a running event store, linked from `Documentation/hosting/production.md`: upgrade every silo; run `cratis chronicle observers initialize`; read each refusal; let jobs complete, or stop them with `--stop-jobs`, knowing that a stopped reactor, webhook or event-store subscription job delivers its completed partitions again and has to be named in `--accept-duplicates`; the downgrade floor from that point on; and that a store that is never initialized keeps today's behavior, including its silent loss on type-set changes. `Documentation/concepts/observers.mdx` and `Documentation/hosting/configuration/observers.md` describe the initialization states.

## Options considered

- **Readers that run across the change with a revision cutover.** Not provable against the in-memory tail and callback side effects. Rejected.
- **Job status as the drain barrier.** Orleans persists `Stopped` before the steps stop. Rejected for `StopAndDrain`.
- **Awaiting a drain inside the observer's turn.** Step work and job hooks call the non-reentrant observer, so the two block each other. Rejected for polling with `TimeSpan.Zero` from a timer.
- **Draining through `IJobs.StopAndDrain` and taking `NotFound` as gone.** Its `NotFound` comes from job storage, which does not know a job whose `Start` passed admission but has not written yet. Rejected for draining through the job grain by the identity registered before `Start`.
- **An atomic admission reservation in Cratis.Orleans.** Would make admission and the first write one step. Not needed: a non-reentrant job grain already orders a drain after a `Start` in its turn, and a `Start` after the drain asks admission again. Not requested.
- **One non-interleaved commit turn as the guard against late callbacks.** `[AlwaysInterleave]` callbacks run at any await of it. Rejected for no-ops during the change, the synchronous latch and re-checks after every await.
- **A progress record per handled batch.** About a hundred times the step writes of every reactor and webhook job, permanently, for a rare event. Rejected for records at durable checkpoints and outcomes.
- **Retaining succeeded step outcomes in Cratis.Orleans** (opt-in retention with a step-outcomes query). Would make progress records unnecessary, but is not in 1.21. Deferred to an Orleans issue if the records prove costly.
- **Keeping replacement jobs after completion until the observer acknowledges them** (`KeepAfterCompleted`). Works for replays only and leaves the retention to each job type. Rejected for a terminal row written by every job, deleted only after its obligation is consumed.
- **Continuations, or the job list, inside the control record.** One document and one turn grow with the partition or job count. Rejected for rows.
- **Carrying an unresolved start's plan with its old job id.** Collides with the execution that may exist (`AlreadyBeenPrepared`) or duplicates it beside its harvested plan. Rejected for one reconciled successor with a fresh id.
- **Deferring a quarantined observer's change until the quarantine ends.** The window below the fence becomes unbounded and silent. Rejected.
- **Initialization as a quiesce, run automatically at the end of the rollout.** Every observer would wait out its timeout at once, and jobs from the previous release would have to be settled in place. Rejected for the operator's initialize observers.
- **Settling jobs from before the gate in place** (an allowlist of them, a boundary, and step accounting with an operator's redelivery decision; rounds 5 and 6). Their coverage cannot be reconstructed reliably: Orleans rebuilds a job's step count from surviving steps, and a namespace baseline recorded during a mixed rollout can be stale. Six review rounds kept finding new edges. Rejected for no coexistence (decided by L0 on the decider's delegation).
- **Dropping the change-in-place for side-effecting observers** (a type change on a reactor needs a new observer id). Keeps the hardest case out but gives up the rolling-deployment fix the decision exists for. Rejected.
- **Enabling automatically once every silo carries the marker.** Would start new behavior without anyone choosing to and would have to settle jobs from before it. Rejected for the explicit step; the marker remains its precondition.
- **Enabling the quiesce on Chronicle's own resume guards before Orleans admission.** Covers only the resume paths that reach `CanResume` or Chronicle's code. Rejected.
- **Fencing abandoned subscriber calls.** Would hold every retry, not only a change, until the client answers or disconnects: delivery fencing for the whole engine. Left to #5014 and decision 0004.

## Default if unanswered

Decision 0010 cannot be enabled, so events of an added type keep being lost silently whenever versions alternate and clients with different sets keep replacing each other. Enabling 0010 on a weaker mechanism would let a reader cross the change and skip what this record guarantees it cannot.

## Timeline and scope

The record ships with decision 0010 in the delivery order of section 9, and its engine slice cannot merge before Chronicle takes a Cratis.Orleans 1.21 release. Every new-behavior specification is red before its change.

1. **State and storage** (in slice 2): the intent fields, `ChangingDefinition` and `Initializing`, the progress-record store, the continuation, restart-plan and inventory rows with conditional state updates, terminal rows, the event-store and observer initialization state, and the downgrade refusal, on every provider. *Verify:* round trips on InMemory, MongoDB and SQL; conditional updates never move a row backwards; the refusal spec in [Verification](#verification).
2. **Contracts** (slice 3): complete definition change, initialize observers with its per-observer results and reasons, and the definition-change and initialization state in the observer's read model.
3. **Engine** (slice 5): entering a change, the inventory and its registration before `Start`, drain through the grain, progress records, terminal rows and their consumption order, harvest, successor reconciliation, commit and resume, revisions on jobs, callbacks and batches, the callback rules of section 5, Chronicle's `MayRun` with its pass-through and the step-start check, forced completion with its reason and audit event, metrics and alerts. *Verify:* Core.Specs and integration specs in `Integration/Client/for_Observers/` and `Integration/Clustering/`.
4. **Enablement** (slice 7): the marker grain type with admission, initialize observers with the cluster check, per-observer refusals, `--stop-jobs` and `--accept-duplicates`, initialization from the namespace's own registration, serialization with definition changes, and initialization at creation. *Verify:* the enablement specs.

**Out of scope:** fencing abandoned subscriber calls (#5014); retaining succeeded step outcomes in Cratis.Orleans; an automated rollout with an older Chronicle binary in the same cluster; running decision 0010 alongside observer jobs started before it; recovering events lost before initialization.

## Open questions

None of the design is open. One dependency is outside this repository: section 2 relies on job grains not being reentrant and on `IJob` and `IJobDrain` having no interleaving methods, which holds in Cratis.Orleans 1.21 but is pinned there only by the absence of attributes. Cratis.Orleans is asked to pin it by specification; if it ever changes, draining through the grain stops being proof and this record has to be revisited.

Answered:

- **Coexistence with state from before** is ruled out (decided by L0 on the decider's delegation): enablement is an explicit, idempotent operator step that refuses while any silo is older or any observer job from before exists; stopping such a job restarts catch-up or replay from the observer's recorded position, and duplicates for side-effecting observers are surfaced and accepted per observer (section 9).
- **A membership change restarts a running replay.** The replayed prefix is delivered again in replay mode; deferring the change until the replay ends would instead exclude events of the added type appended meanwhile. Restart plans keep their finite bounds, are started at most once (sections 3 and 4), keep catch-up out of what they own until they are consumed, and a held or failed replay clears no fence (decision 0010, section 5).
- **Forced completion** uses the existing authorization with a required reason and an audit event shown in the Workbench; operator roles are Cratis/Chronicle#5015 (section 8).
- **The downgrade refusal** from the state slice on is taken: the gate release is the downgrade floor once a store is initialized; there is no bridge release (section 9).
- **The subscriber-timeout overlap** is accepted because it is pre-existing and this decision does not widen it; it is tracked in Cratis/Chronicle#5014 (section 8).
- **The quiesce waits for Orleans owner admission:** initialization refuses until every silo runs a release that registers it on Cratis.Orleans 1.21 or later (section 9).

## Verification

**Done when:**

- until an event store is initialized, its observers behave exactly as today, nothing new is written for it, and no upgrade waits for initialization;
- initialize observers refuses, with every reason per observer, while any silo is older or any observer job from before exists, and is idempotent;
- no reader of an older revision runs or advances progress after a commit, no job of the key runs while a change is pending, and no job from before initialization runs after it;
- registration transitions add no duplicate delivery, except through forced completion and through jobs the operator stopped at initialization; at-least-once redelivery after a crash or timeout is unchanged, because subscriber effects precede the durable outcome (`Observer.Handling.cs:255, 331-357`); a delivery abandoned by the subscriber timeout may overlap its partition's retry, as today (#5014);
- every obligation has exactly one successor after a change, and no ended job is started again;
- a change that cannot drain is visible and completes through an operator command without editing storage.

**Verify by** these specifications, each red before its change:

- **No change before initialization:** with every silo on the gate release and the event store not initialized, a narrower registration replaces the set, the #4987 hand-over applies, admission admits every job, no control record, row or record is written, and retire type answers `NotInitialized`.
- **Initialize refusals:** with a silo without the marker (simulated in `ClusteringFixture`, which runs one binary in process, `Integration/Clustering/ClusteringFixture.cs:22-33`) or a missing manifest, the event store is refused and the silo is named; with an unfinished catch-up on a reactor and a replay on a projection, both observers are refused with their job ids, statuses and consequences; a second run with nothing changed answers the same and writes nothing more; after the jobs complete, a run initializes both and a further run answers `AlreadyInitialized`.
- **Stopping jobs at initialization:** `--stop-jobs` stops a projection's catch-up and initializes it, and its catch-up restarts from its recorded `Next`; a reactor's job is stopped only when the reactor is named in `--accept-duplicates`, and otherwise stays refused with the duplicate reason; while `Initializing` no job of the key is admitted and the watchdog starts none; a crash while `Initializing` continues on reactivation; the audit event lists stopped jobs and accepted duplicates.
- **Initialization source:** namespace X registers `[A, E]` and writes it to the shared definition; namespace Y, registered with `[A]`, initializes with members `[A]`, and `E` joins Y only through a change with a fence; an observer with no current registration answers `AwaitingRegistration` and initializes with its next registration; a failed partition from before retries in live mode from its last attempt and gets its owner at its first recovery job.
- **Serialized with definition changes:** a webhook filter change racing initialization of its namespace observers either completes before the contract is frozen, and the namespaces initialize with it, or is rejected after the freeze before anything is stored; a reactor registration racing its observer's initialization is either the registration it initializes from or is checked against the contract it initialized with.
- **Slipped start:** a `Start` sent before initialization whose first write lands after the job check is refused at `Resume`, its steps stop at their start, nothing of it delivers, and the observer deletes it.
- **Created initialized:** an event store created on a cluster where every silo carries the marker, and a namespace created in an initialized store, are initialized at creation; their observers initialize at their first registration.
- **Status:** the Workbench and `cratis chronicle observers show` give each initialization state and the latest refusal reasons.
- **Drain barrier:** a step still writing its checkpoint when its job is persisted `Stopped` is not harvested until `StopAndDrain` answers `Drained`; `TimedOut` is polled again on a later tick; the observer never awaits a drain inside its turn; a step's subscriber wait finishes before it stops.
- **In-flight start:** a revision-*r* `Start` admitted before the intent, whose first write stalls past both enumerations and past the observer's call timeout, is drained through its grain after its turn, and no step of it delivers after the commit; the same `Start` arriving after a drain that answered `Drained` for a never-started grain is refused; an `IJobs.StopAndDrain` answer of `NotFound` is never taken as drained.
- **Row lifecycle:** an empty replay that writes its terminal row inside `Start` keeps it *terminal* when `Start` answers; a crash after each of the four consumption steps, for a completion call, the watchdog, the resume and the harvest, leaves the plan either unconsumed with its terminal row present or consumed, and its job is never started again; a terminal row of an older revision found after a commit is deleted without being applied.
- **Successor reconciliation:** a restart plan of *r + 1* whose `Start` ran and lost its answer is drained by the change to *r + 2* and becomes one plan of *r + 2* with a new job id, and the stopped job is deleted after the commit; one whose job completed is settled by its terminal row; one whose `Start` never ran is carried with a new id, and the late `Start` is refused; the same three cases for a continuation; never two plans for one obligation.
- **Many jobs:** a key with 10,000 failed partitions, each with a running retry job, drains with at most 100 drain calls and 1,000 row operations per tick, its control record does not grow with the job count, and registrations are handled between ticks.
- **Interleaving callbacks:** a `CaughtUp` suspended at its state write when a change begins, resuming after the commit, neither enters `Routing` nor clears revision *r + 1*'s markers; `RegisterCatchingUpPartitions` and the three recovery callbacks change nothing during a change; a revision-*r* call resuming at any await of the commit turn changes nothing.
- **Stale results:** a `CaughtUp`, retry completion, `ReportHandledEvents`, `PartitionFailed` or queue batch of an older revision arriving after the commit changes nothing, and its loss leaves no partition repeated; a completion call whose terminal row was already consumed changes nothing; one that never arrives is consumed from its row by the watchdog.
- **Harvest:** a catch-up stopped mid-partition continues from its last durable position; a partition whose step succeeded, with its step state deleted and `OnStepCompletedOrStopped` never run, is not repeated; the same for a retry; a job that completed just before the change, whose `CaughtUp` became a no-op, loses nothing; a step that handled 70 and failed at 71 resumes from its entry at 71 with no continuation; a failed terminal-record write closes the step instead of reporting success; a failing terminal-row write leaves the job `Failed` with its state; a step writes one record per durable checkpoint and one per outcome.
- **Large key:** a harvest of 100,000 partitions spreads over ticks, the control record's size does not depend on the partition count, and registrations are handled between ticks.
- **Join position:** an `E` event committed to storage but not yet in the grain's tail lies below `J`; an append in flight during the tail read is delivered; an empty sequence fences at `First`; the tail is read from the primary.
- **Missed events:** `E` events appended between `J0` and `J` raise the alert with their count.
- **Quarantine:** a change while quarantined drains, commits with its fences at that commit's `J`, stays quarantined, and resumes when the quarantine is cleared.
- **Watchdog:** during a change it neither unsubscribes, rescues jobs nor fast-forwards; it alerts.
- **Admission,** against Cratis.Orleans 1.21: an old-revision job is refused on explicit `Resume`, on rehydration while `Running` (stopped with a pending drain, no step work), and at a stage advance; a job of the current revision is refused while a change is pending; a job with a terminal row is refused at `Start`; a job without a revision is refused once its observer is initializing or initialized; a throwing `MayRun` leaves the job unchanged and not running; the observer never awaits a refusal in its turn.
- **Replay restart:** a change during replay restarts it with the new set; a bounded, selected-type partition replay restarts with its bounds and remaining types; a crash after the commit, before or after the replacement job is stored, starts it exactly once; a replacement that completes, and is deleted by Orleans, before its plan is removed is not started again, including an empty replay that completes inside its `Start`; a failed replacement clears no fence.
- **Bounded and recoverable:** a subscriber that never answers stops being waited for at the subscriber timeout and its partition fails; a job that never drains raises the alert after `DefinitionChangeTimeout`; forced completion is refused without a reason, and with one appends its audit event and commits; a silo crash before, during and after the commit converges, including a crash mid-drain, which Orleans completes on reactivation without resuming the job.
- **Gate marker:** `AddChronicleToSilo` never registers the marker without admission.
- **Downgrade refusal:** a silo of a release from the state slice on that finds an initialized control record refuses to activate the observer. This specification passes or fails.
- **Sweep:** removes records of vanished jobs older than the response timeout, consumes terminal rows whose call was lost, keeps rows of a pending change, and never runs during a change.

## Consequences

- **Easier:** decision 0010's changes are applied without a reader crossing them, and the argument rests on two engine guarantees with their own specifications rather than on timing; work started from a continuation or plan survives any number of later changes, each with one successor; nothing from before the decision runs under it, so no migration of in-flight work has to be proven; an upgrade alone never changes behavior.
- **Harder:** a running event store needs an operator to initialize it after the upgrade, and stays on today's behavior until then; jobs from before have to complete or be stopped first, and stopping a reactor's, webhook's or event-store subscription's job delivers its completed partitions again, which the operator accepts per observer; a change pauses that namespace's observer while it drains; the release waits for Cratis.Orleans 1.21; jobs, callbacks and queue batches carry revisions; every job start writes an inventory row first and every job end a terminal row; steps write progress records; an initialized store has the gate release as its downgrade floor.
- **Ruled out:** readers that span a membership change; running decision 0010 alongside jobs from before it; enabling it without an operator's step on a running store; taking storage `NotFound` as proof that a job cannot run; silent redelivery of a side-effecting observer's work at initialization.
