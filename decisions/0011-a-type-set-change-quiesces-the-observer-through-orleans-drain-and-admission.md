# A change to an observer's event-type set quiesces the observer through the Cratis.Orleans drain and owner admission, and is enabled only once every silo runs that admission

Proposed for Cratis/Chronicle#4955 and Cratis/Chronicle#4987 as the mechanism [decision 0010](0010-observer-event-types-are-the-union-of-registered-definitions.md) uses to apply a change to an observer's members; the two are proposed together and neither is in force without the other. It rests on two Cratis.Orleans guarantees: the drain barrier `StopAndDrain` (v1.20.0, which Chronicle runs) and owner admission (Cratis/Orleans#81, stage 2b, released in v1.21.0). Open points are under [Open questions](#open-questions).

Paths are relative to `Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/` unless they start with `Source/`, `Integration/` or `Documentation/`; paths marked *Orleans* are in Cratis/Orleans at `v1.20.0`, and those marked *Orleans 1.21* at `v1.21.0`; `Documentation/concepts/jobs.md` there is cited as *jobs.md*. Chronicle line numbers are at `bec5709f8c` (release 19.77.2).

## Context

A reader that runs across a membership change cannot be proven harmless against this code. The event sequence grain advances its in-memory tail after the storage commit (`Source/Kernel/Core/EventSequences/EventSequence.cs:206, 827-831`), so an old reader can pass an event the join position is computed below; old callbacks clear catch-up markers and transition state, not just positions; resumed jobs keep their old type set (`JobsManagerExtensions.cs:124-152`). Decision 0010 therefore needs the reader removed before a change, not reasoned about. The code it has to be removed from:

- **Stopping is not stopped.** Orleans persists `Stopped` before the steps stop. `StopAndDrain` (jobs.md, "Stop a job and wait until it is drained") is the acknowledged barrier, but it does not cover calls nobody awaits, such as the fire-and-forget `CaughtUp` (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:60-72`), and it does not keep a succeeded step's record: persisting a success deletes the step's state, and after a crash `OnStepCompletedOrStopped` can run twice or not at all.
- **Successful jobs disappear.** A job that completes successfully clears its stored state unless it keeps it (`KeepAfterCompleted`, false by default, *Orleans 1.21* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/Job.cs:62, 621-630`); neither the catch-up nor the replay job keeps it. A job's id is therefore not a durable record that it ran.
- **Admission precedes the first write.** `Start` asks admission and only then writes the job (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:157, 175`). Until that write lands, job storage does not know the job, and `IJobs.StopAndDrain` answers `NotFound` from storage without reaching the grain (*Orleans 1.21* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/Jobs.cs:94-99`).
- **The observer interleaves.** The grain is not reentrant, but about twenty `IObserver` methods are `[AlwaysInterleave]` (`IObserver.cs`), among them the three `CaughtUp` overloads (`:456-476`), `RegisterCatchingUpPartitions` (`:447`) and `FailedPartitionRecovered`, `FailedPartitionNotRecovered` and `FailedPartitionPartiallyRecovered` (`:343-374`). They run at any await of any turn: a `CaughtUp` clears the catch-up markers (`Observer.Catchup.cs:170`), awaits its state write (`:181`) and then enters `Routing` (`:186-189`).
- **Checkpoints are debounced and steps are many.** A step's checkpoint becomes durable every `StepCheckpointBatchInterval` batches, 100 by default (`Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/Jobs.cs:51`), except for collapsing projections (`Jobs/HandleEventsForPartition.cs:69`); a reactor catch-up has one step per partition (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:115-172`), and a key can have a recovery job per failed partition.
- **Catch-up steps are unbounded.** They read to `EventSequenceNumber.Max` (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:170`, `Jobs/RetryFailedPartition.cs:121`); a partition appended to faster than it is handled never ends its step.
- **Orleans 1.20 does not ask the owner on every path.** It resumes a job left `Running` or `StartingSteps` without calling `CanResume` (*Orleans* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/Job.cs:176-226`, `:396`), and advances stages without asking.
- **Unsubscribing pauses jobs.** `Unsubscribe` stops every non-replay job of the key (`Observer.cs:455-461`, `PauseJobs` `:1034-1048`), and only `ResumeJobs` (`:1155-1181`) resumes them.
- **Jobs started by the previous release carry no revision,** and steps that succeeded under it have left nothing but their contribution to their job's aggregate handover (`Jobs/CatchUpObserver.cs:52-72`).
- **Every labeled merge to `main` releases,** so a multi-slice change ships as several releases.

## Decision

A change that adds a type to, or retires a type from, a namespace observer's members (decision 0010) runs as a **quiesce**: the observer stops admitting work, drains every job of its key through the job grain itself (each job is registered in a durable job inventory before its `Start` is sent), harvests positions from Chronicle's own progress and terminal records into per-partition rows, carries every unconsumed obligation of the previous revision forward, reads the committed tail from storage as the join position, and commits the next revision; only then does work resume. Every call from a job carries the revision it was created under and cannot move observer state across the change. Owner admission in Cratis.Orleans 1.21 keeps every job of an older revision, and every job that has already ended, from running again. The quiesce, and decision 0010 with it, are enabled only on a cluster where every silo runs a Chronicle release that registers admission. Observers are initialized without a quiesce from a baseline their own namespace recorded, and the first change of a reactor, webhook or event-store subscription settles the jobs it inherited from the previous release through an explicit legacy settlement that never redelivers without an operator's decision.

### 1. Entering a change

- **Triggers:** a registration that declares a type that is not a member, and a retirement that passes its checks (decision 0010, sections 3 and 6). Initialization is not a change (section 9).
- **Latch first.** In the turn that starts the change, before its first await, the observer sets an in-memory latch and its running state to `ChangingDefinition`. It then writes durably the pending change, the running state and the committed tail `J0` (section 4). Its job inventory (section 2) is already durable; the change's enumerations add to it.
- **Stop admitting work.** The queue subscription is removed (leaving `Observing`), and `Handle` already drops batches while the observer is not `Active` (`Observer.Handling.cs:394`). Catch-up, partition recovery, retry reminders and replay requests are refused, and admission refuses the key's jobs (section 6). The watchdog only alerts: no unsubscribe on disconnect (`Observer.Watchdog.cs:170-212`; decision 0010's target removal still applies), no job rescue (`CheckJobTasks`, `:217-262`), no fast-forward (`:343-369`).
- **Quarantine does not defer a change.** A quarantined observer runs the change the same way: its jobs are drained whether stopped or not, and replays, which `PauseJobs` leaves running, are drained too. After the commit it stays `Quarantined`, and resumes when the quarantine ends. Deferring the change until then would put every event of an added type appended meanwhile below its fence, for as long as the quarantine lasts. The one exception is a first change whose legacy settlement cannot account for inherited work (section 9): it waits, alerting, for the quarantine to end or for an operator's redelivery decision, because running inherited work on a quarantined observer and redelivering it silently are both worse. It is never a silent wait.

### 2. The job inventory and draining

- **The job inventory** is a set of rows keyed by observer key and job id, each with the job type, its kind (catch-up, partition catch-up, retry, hold resume, replay, legacy), its revision, the continuation or restart-plan row it was started from, if any, and its state: *intended*, *started* or *terminal* with an outcome (section 3). Rows are written and read in pages, at most `Observers.DefinitionChangeRowsPerTick` (1,000 by default) per tick, like continuations; neither a turn nor a stored document grows with the number of jobs.
- **Registered before `Start`.** For an initialized observer, a job's row is written as *intended*, in the observer's turn and before `Start` is dispatched, with the job id the observer chose (`IJobs.Start(key, jobId, request)`, *Orleans* `Source/Orleans/Jobs/IJobs.cs:34`). It becomes *started* when `Start` answers. A `Start` whose answer is lost or timed out leaves the row *intended*: it still counts in the quiesce until it is drained.
- **Drained through the grain.** A non-interleaving grain timer ticks every `Observers.DefinitionChangePollInterval` (1 second by default) and calls `StopAndDrain(TimeSpan.Zero)` on the job grain itself, addressed by the row's job type and id (`IGrainFactory.GetGrain<TJob>(jobId, key)` as `IJobDrain`), for rows not yet drained, at most `Observers.DefinitionChangeCallsPerTick` (100 by default) per tick and round-robin across ticks. It does not use `IJobs.StopAndDrain`, whose `NotFound` comes from storage. Job grains are not reentrant and neither `IJob` nor `IJobDrain` interleaves, so a drain that reaches a grain while its `Start` is in its turn runs after that turn has written the job and fanned out its steps, and drains them; a drain that reaches the grain first finds no execution and answers `Drained` (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.Drain.cs:69-72`), and the `Start` that arrives afterwards asks admission, which refuses it because the intent is durable (section 6). The grain's own answer is the proof that nothing of the job runs; storage `NotFound` is not.
- `Drained` and `Completed` count as drained; `TimedOut` and an error are polled again on a later tick. The observer never awaits a drain with a positive timeout: step work and job hooks call the observer's non-interleaving `ReportHandledEvents` and `PartitionFailed`, so a waiting drain and a waiting step would block each other (jobs.md, "Do not wait for a drain from a grain your job calls back into").
- **Enumeration** finds jobs no row names: those started before initialization, by a release before the engine, or as legacy executions (section 9). The key's unfinished jobs are enumerated in pages when the change starts and once more when every row has drained; each job found gets a row and is drained. A job started before initialization had no intended row, but its job state is written before any of its steps starts (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:175`), and a step of a job without a revision checks admission when it starts (section 6). A step that started before the intent was durable therefore belongs to a job the final enumeration sees, and one that starts later stops without reading.
- `Drained` means no step's work or activation callback runs any more, each step's outcome and last checkpoint is in step storage, and the job's hooks have run. A step does not pass its cancellation into the subscriber wait (`Jobs/HandleEventsForPartition.cs:435`, `ObserverSubscriberExtensions.cs:33-59`), so it finishes the wait it is in, with an answer or the subscriber timeout, before it stops; a drain never abandons a subscriber wait.

### 3. Progress records, terminal rows, the harvest and carried obligations

- **When a step writes a progress record.** A step of an observer whose control record is initialized writes a progress record (observer key, job id, partition, position, outcome) when its checkpoint has become durable (the debounced write in `ReportNewSuccessfullyHandledEvent`, `Jobs/HandleEventsForPartition.cs:101`) and at its terminal outcome: success, stop, failure or hold. That is one write per durable checkpoint and one per outcome, not one per batch.
- **A succeeded step's position** is the one it reports with success. Its record is written before it reports; if that write fails, the step does not report success and closes with a partial result, as a refused checkpoint callback already does (`ReportFromWork`, `ClosedResult`, `:404-417`). `Drained` guarantees every outcome of a drained step was reported, so its terminal record exists.
- **Terminal rows.** A job of an initialized observer, revisioned or legacy, records its outcome in its inventory row (adding the row if enumeration has not) as the first thing in `OnAllStepsCompleted`, before `CaughtUp`, `Replayed` or a recovery callback is sent: the catch-up handover (last handled, last scanned, whether every step succeeded), the replay's outcome, or the recovery's result. Orleans calls that hook before it clears a completed job's state and keeps a job whose hook throws as `Failed` (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:612-630`), so a job never disappears without its terminal row or its stored state. The observer applies a terminal row once, through the completion call outside a change or through the harvest during one, and deletes it with the job's progress records when it has applied it; a completion call whose row is gone changes nothing.
- **The harvested position** of a partition is the later of its record and its step's checkpoint in step storage.
- **Every record of the key is harvested,** not only those of the recorded jobs, and so is every terminal row, so a job that completed just before the change, whose `CaughtUp` the change turns into a no-op (section 5), loses nothing. A crash between applying a terminal row and deleting it applies it again, which moves no position backwards.
- **A failed entry beats a continuation.** A partition with a failed-partition entry, a failure or a hold, gets no continuation: a step that handled up to 70 and failed at 71 resumes from its entry at 71, as `ProbeAndStartCatchupJob` already skips failed partitions (`Observer.Catchup.cs:281`). A terminal failure record becomes a failed entry at its position when the observer has none, so a `PartitionFailed` still on its way adds nothing. A recovery job's record moves its entry's resume position only when the owner matches (decision 0010, section 5).
- **What a record becomes.** Catch-up and partition catch-up records become partition **continuations**; retry and hold-resume records become the failed partition's resume position. Replays are not harvested; each becomes a **restart plan**: its scope (the whole observer or one partition), its bounds, its event types (a full replay takes the new member set; a partition replay keeps its requested types that are still members) and the id its replacement job will have.
- **Unconsumed rows are obligations, carried.** A continuation or restart plan of the current revision *r* whose job has not been started is the only durable record of that work, so the harvest of the change to *r + 1* carries it before the commit: a continuation becomes a continuation of *r + 1* at the later of its position and anything its job recorded, unless a failed entry now owns the partition; a restart plan keeps its scope, bounds and job id, takes the new member set if it is a full replay, and keeps its requested types that are still members if it is a partition replay, clearing without a job when none remain. A row whose job is *started* has been transferred: that job is drained and harvested like any other, and its origin row is removed with it.
- **Rows, not one document.** Continuations, restart plans and the job inventory are rows keyed by observer, revision and partition, plan or job, written over successive ticks, at most `Observers.DefinitionChangeRowsPerTick` per tick, so neither a turn nor a stored document grows with the partition or job count. Readers take rows of the current revision. Rows of revision *r + 1* belong to the pending change and are kept while it is pending; rows of an older revision are removed only after the commit that carried them. A crash mid-harvest re-enters from the intent and rewrites rows by key.
- **Sweep.** Outside a change, the watchdog tick applies a terminal row whose completion call has not arrived within the Orleans response timeout, as that call would have, and removes progress records whose job has neither a row nor stored state and that are older than that timeout. It never runs during a change.

### 4. Commit and resume

- **The commit turn** runs on the timer once every row is written. Before its first await it advances the in-memory revision to *r + 1*, so a revision-*r* call that resumes at any later await finds a newer revision (section 5). It waits for any observer state write already in flight, so a write prepared under revision *r* cannot land after the commit's.
- **Join position.** It reads the committed tail from storage (`IEventSequenceStorage.GetTailSequenceNumber`, `Source/Kernel/Storage/EventSequences/IEventSequenceStorage.cs:220`) on the primary: MongoDB primary read preference with majority read concern, the SQL primary. Each added type's fence is `J = tail + 1`, or `EventSequenceNumber.First` when the sequence is empty (`Unavailable` survives addition, `Source/Kernel/Concepts/Events/EventSequenceNumber.cs:15-20, 97`).
- **One write** of the control record publishes revision *r + 1*, the members with their fences, the registration identities and retirement results, and any settlement decision (section 9), and clears the pending change (decision 0010, section 2). Then the old jobs, their applied terminal rows and harvested progress records, and the rows of revision *r* are deleted.
- **Missed events are counted, not owed.** Events of an added type with sequence numbers from `J0` up to `J` were appended while the change ran and lie below the fence. When there are any, a warning alert gives their count and recommends a replay; no per-type debt is recorded.
- **Restart plans first.** For each plan: a terminal row for its job means the replay already ended, and its outcome is applied (a successful full replay clears every fence; a failed or partial one clears none) and the plan and row are removed. Otherwise the plan's inventory row is written as *intended* and its job started with its recorded id. Admission refuses a `Start` for a job id that has a terminal row (section 6), and the job's terminal row is written in the job grain's turn before it clears its state, so a crash anywhere in this sequence, including after the replacement completed and Orleans deleted it, and including an empty replay that completes inside its own `Start`, starts the job at most once. While a full-replay plan is unconsumed the observer starts no catch-up and subscribes no live delivery; a partition-replay plan keeps its partition out of catch-up.
- **Then the rest.** The queue is subscribed with the new set and revision; catch-up runs from `Next` and excludes partitions with continuations, which get partition catch-ups from their continuation (`Observer.Catchup.cs:313-321`), each registered in the inventory before its `Start`; retries run from their failed partitions; holds whose awaited type is now declared or retired resume. A continuation's row is removed when its job is *started*. An observer that is quarantined stays so and resumes when the quarantine ends.

### 5. Calls from jobs carry the revision

- Job requests, live queue batches and every call from step work or a job hook to the observer carry the revision they were created under; a job without a revision counts as revision 1 (section 9). Completion calls carry the job id and are applied only through the job's terminal row (section 3).
- **During a change** the job calls that advance positions or clear state are no-ops, logged: the three `CaughtUp` overloads, `RegisterCatchingUpPartitions`, `FailedPartitionRecovered`, `FailedPartitionPartiallyRecovered` and `FailedPartitionNotRecovered`. The harvest carries what they would have carried. `PartitionFailed` and `ReportHandledEvents`, which do not interleave, still apply; a transition they would trigger waits for the resume.
- **Interleaving callbacks re-check after every await.** If the latch has moved since the call entered, because a change began or the revision advanced, the callback stops without a further mutation or transition. What it did before its first await belonged to the revision that was current then. A `CaughtUp` that cleared markers before a change and resumes after its commit therefore never reaches `TransitionTo<Routing>()` and cannot clear the markers of revision *r + 1*'s partition catch-ups (`Observer.Catchup.cs:317`).
- **After the commit** a call or batch of an older revision is ignored entirely: it advances no position, clears no marker and triggers no transition.

### 6. Owner admission (Cratis.Orleans 1.21)

A drain stops a job; it does not decide whether the job may run again. Chronicle uses the admission API of Cratis/Orleans#81, stage 2b:

- `IJobAdmission.MayRun(JobAdmissionRequest(JobKey, JobId, JobType, IJobRequest), CancellationToken)` returns `Task<bool>` and is registered with `siloBuilder.AddJobAdmission<T>()`, a singleton added with `TryAddEnumerable`. A 1.21 silo without the registration admits everything.
- It is asked at `Start` (refusal: `StartJobError.NotAdmitted`, 7), at every `Resume` — explicit, rehydration of a job left running or starting, and recovery — (refusal: `CannotResumeJobError.NotAdmitted`, 3; `IJobsManager.Resume` returns false) and at every stage advance. A preparing job is not asked on `Resume`, which starts nothing there; its `Start` was asked.
- Refusing a `Running` or `StartingSteps` job makes `Resume` write `Stopped` with a pending drain and answer `NotAdmitted` at once; the drain completes in the job's own timer turn. If that drain times out, the job stays stopped with the drain pending until the owner's `StopAndDrain` or its next activation; nothing restarts it. A refused stage advance stops the job and runs `OnStopped`.
- A refusal is final for that attempt; recovery and rehydration go through `Resume`, which asks again, so a job stays refused for as long as the owner refuses.
- Throwing, being cancelled or exceeding the time limit (the 1.20 drain bound, about half the Orleans response timeout) fails the operation and changes nothing; it is never a refusal. Rehydration leaves the job `Running` in storage and asks on the next pass; a stage advance retries the check on a timer.
- Orleans has no per-step check, and none is needed: step work starts only from the fan-out of an admitted `Start`, `Resume` or stage start, and a restart after an earlier stop or a deferred start happens only inside an admitted execution that has not been stopped. A job type with `StartStepsInBackground` is asked in `Start`'s turn and fans out on the next timer tick; only `Stop`, `StopAndDrain` or `Remove` can run in between, and each makes that tick a no-op.
- `MayRun` never calls the job, its steps, or a non-interleaving method of a grain that may be awaiting that job's `Start` or `Resume`.

Chronicle's `MayRun` reads the control record and the job's inventory row from storage on the primary and calls no grain. It decides in this order:

1. **Out of scope or not initialized: admit.** A job of an observer kind decision 0010 leaves unchanged, or of an observer whose control record is not initialized, is admitted as today. This keeps the engine slice, which registers admission long before the gate, inert.
2. **The job has a terminal row: refuse.** A job that has ended never begins another execution under the same id (section 4).
3. **No revision (legacy):** admitted while revision 1 is current and no change is pending. While a change is pending, admitted only when it is on that change's settlement allowlist, the settlement is running and the observer is not quarantined (section 9). Otherwise refused, and after the first change commits, always refused.
4. **With a revision:** admitted when it is the current revision and no change is pending and, for a recovery job, its owner matches the failed-partition entry.

**Steps of a job without a revision check at their start.** Step work of a legacy job evaluates the same predicate when it starts and stops without reading if the job would be refused; for an allowlisted job under settlement it reads no further than the settlement boundary (section 9). It is Chronicle's own step code, not an Orleans check, and it exists because such a job may have been admitted before its observer kept an inventory. It costs one read per step of legacy jobs only.

The observer never awaits a refusal's drain in its own turn: `ResumeJobs` and the revision filter in `JobsManagerExtensions.cs:124-152` skip jobs of other revisions, so a job that admission would refuse while running reaches `MayRun` only through rehydration, outside the observer. Those filters, `CanResume` overrides that apply the same predicate, and deleting old jobs on sight are defense in depth; on their own they leave the paths Orleans 1.20 does not route through `CanResume` unguarded.

### 7. Why no reader crosses a change

Every job that can run for the key is in the inventory before its `Start` is sent, or was written before any of its steps started and is found by the final enumeration; a `Start` admitted under revision *r* whose first write stalls past both enumerations is drained through its grain after its turn, or is refused when it arrives after a drain that found nothing (section 2). Once a job answers `Drained`, none of its steps reads, delivers or checkpoints again, and admission keeps it and every other job of revision *r* from running. `J` is read only after every job of the key has drained, so every position a revision-*r* reader scanned or passed held an event already committed and lies below `J`; whatever it skipped of an added type is history below the join, which decision 0010 excludes. Reading the committed tail from storage on the primary is what makes this hold: an event committed but not yet in the grain's in-memory tail is below `J`, and a lagging secondary cannot put `J` below an event an old reader passed. Appends are numbered and committed serially by the event sequence grain (`Source/Kernel/Core/EventSequences/EventSequence.cs:793-831`), so an append in flight during the tail read gets a number at or above `J` and is delivered. Every reader after the commit runs with revision *r + 1*, the full member set and the fences, and section 5 keeps a late revision-*r* call from moving anything. That the harvest spans several ticks changes none of this: nothing it reads can change once the jobs are drained.

The one reader of an older revision that runs while a change is pending is an allowlisted legacy execution under settlement (section 9). It reads its own request's types, no further than the settlement boundary `B`, which is at or below `J`; its callbacks are no-ops and its outcome reaches the observer only through its terminal row; and it has completed or drained before `J` is read. It therefore does not cross the change either: it ends inside it.

### 8. Bounded, observable, recoverable

- **Pause.** With jobs running, a drain settles within the subscriber timeout (`Observers.SubscriberTimeout`, 30 seconds by default, `Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/Observers.cs:34`) plus `JobsOptions.StepStopTimeout` (10 seconds) of being requested, and a later tick sees it; the inventory adds a tick per 100 drain calls and the harvest a tick per 1,000 rows. A legacy settlement adds the time its executions take to reach `B`. A subscriber timeout at or below zero waits indefinitely, and a drain can stay stuck, for example on a step whose silo cannot drain; then only forced completion ends the pause.
- **Visible.** `Observers.DefinitionChangeTimeout` (5 minutes by default) raises a warning alert when exceeded while polling continues. The running state, the pending change, its age, the jobs still draining, and a legacy settlement's executions, their state and their unaccounted steps appear in the Workbench and the CLI; a settlement waiting for a decision raises its own warning alert. Metrics `chronicle-observer-definition-changes-in-progress` (gauge) and `chronicle-observer-definition-change-duration` (histogram); entry and exit logs carry the revision.
- **Forced completion.** **Complete definition change** with `--force` deletes the remaining jobs without harvesting them and commits; their unharvested progress is delivered again, and an undrained step could deliver out of order until it settles, so it is the one path that weakens per-partition order. On a reactor, webhook or event-store subscription with an unsettled legacy settlement it is also that settlement's redelivery decision (section 9), and its audit event says so. It uses the kernel's existing authorization, which admits any authenticated client including applications (`Source/Kernel/Core/Sequences/ReopenStreamScope.cs:26`); it requires a reason and appends an audit-only system event recording who forced it, when and why, as retirement does, and the Workbench shows that event. Restricting kernel administration operations to an operator role is Cratis/Chronicle#5015.
- **Crashes.** Before the commit the change re-enters from the durable intent; a job already asked to drain completes its drain when next activated and refuses `Resume` until then; inventory rows, progress records, terminal rows, carried rows and the settlement state survive. After the commit, jobs and progress records of older revisions are deleted on sight, restart plans run first, and their terminal rows make every start at most once. No path needs a manual storage edit.
- **Abandoned subscriber calls** are outside the drain. The subscriber timeout abandons the kernel's wait, not the subscriber's work (`ObserverSubscriberExtensions.cs:26-58`, `Reactors/Clients/ReactorObserverSubscriber.cs:56-60`), so a drained observer can still have client work outstanding from a wait abandoned before or during the drain. That partition failed, and its retry after the commit can overlap the abandoned call exactly as a retry after a timeout does today. This decision neither creates nor widens that overlap; it is tracked in Cratis/Chronicle#5014.

### 9. Rollout

- **The cluster gate.** A silo of the gate release registers a marker grain type and `AddJobAdmission<T>()` together in `AddChronicleToSilo` (`Source/Kernel/Grpc/Setup/ChronicleServerSiloBuilderExtensions.cs:48`), and a spec asserts that registering the marker registers admission. That release builds on Cratis.Orleans 1.21, so a silo carrying the marker runs 1.21 or later with admission registered. Each silo reads from the Orleans cluster manifest whether every active silo carries the marker; Chronicle has no silo-capability mechanism of its own (`Source/Kernel/Contracts/Clients/KernelCapabilities.cs` is client-facing). The check needs a complete view of active membership and fails closed: a silo whose manifest is missing counts as older. The quiesce is therefore enabled only once Cratis.Orleans 1.21 has shipped, every silo runs it, and every silo registers admission.
- **Before the gate opens** every silo keeps today's behavior for type changes: the registration replaces the set; no control record, hold, fence, inventory row or progress record is written; retire type, complete definition change, allow legacy redelivery and initialize observer answer `ClusterUpgradeInProgress`. The only new state written before the gate is the namespace baseline below. The gate opens only initialization and is not re-evaluated afterwards.
- **Delaying the gate.** A silo with `Observers.HoldTypeSetGate` set registers admission but not the marker, so the cluster counts it as older and the gate stays closed until the setting is cleared on every silo. It is off by default and lets an operator keep a rollback cheap until the rollout is final.
- **The namespace baseline.** From the state slice on, a silo that applies a subscription of a namespace observer in scope of decision 0010 writes that namespace's **baseline** into the namespace's observer state: the event types, subscriber type and filters the subscription applied. Only the namespace's own observer grain writes it, so another namespace's registrations never reach it, unlike the shared event-store-level definition, which every namespace writes (`ObserverDefinitionGrainStorageProvider.cs:25-26, 42-43`). It is a nullable field that older releases neither read nor overwrite (their MongoDB save is a targeted `$set`; SQL adds a column they do not map), and nothing but initialization reads it.
- **Initialization does not quiesce.** Once the gate is open, the next registration of each namespace observer writes revision 1 directly: the members are the baseline's event types, with no fences, and the compatibility contract (decision 0010, section 4) is the baseline's subscriber type and filters. A registration whose subscriber type or filters differ from the baseline is rejected and the observer stays uninitialized. It changes no set, so the ordinary subscribe path resumes the jobs that unsubscribing paused through `ResumeJobs`, as today. When the registration also declares a type that is not a member, a change starts after the record is written.
- **No baseline.** A namespace observer that has not subscribed under a release from the state slice on has no baseline, and the shared definition is not evidence of what this namespace handled. If the observer is idle — `Next` past the committed tail, no failed partition and no unfinished job — membership before initialization makes no difference to what is delivered, and it initializes with the registration's types and contract. Otherwise it stays uninitialized, behaving as today, with a warning alert *bootstrap decision required*, until an operator runs **initialize observer** naming the event types that namespace already handled: those become members without fences, and any other type the registration declares joins through an ordinary change, with a fence. It is authorized, reasoned and audited as forced completion is.
- **Legacy failed entries.** A failed partition written before initialization has no mode and no owner. It is read as live mode, the only mode today's retries run in (`Jobs/RetryFailedPartition.cs:118-126`), with its resume position at its last attempt, where today's retry starts. Its owner stays empty until the observer starts a recovery job for it, which writes the full owner (decision 0010, section 5). A retry job running for it without a revision is a legacy execution.
- **Legacy executions.** Jobs without a revision were started before initialization; they count as revision 1 and are admitted while revision 1 is current and no change is pending (section 6), and their completion calls apply as today. The first real change after initialization treats them by observer kind:
  - A **replay-safe observer** (projection, reducer or read model) drains them like any job and harvests what their steps recorded. A partition whose step succeeded under the previous release left no record, and Orleans deleted its state, so it is delivered again from `Next` or its failed-partition position: a one-time redelivery, limited to that change.
  - A **reactor, webhook or event-store subscription** does not redeliver without an operator's decision, because their effects are not idempotent (Cratis/Chronicle#4809; an event-store subscription appends to the target's inbox without an idempotency key, `EventStoreSubscriptions/EventStoreSubscriptionObserverSubscriber.cs:92-105`). Its change runs the **legacy settlement** below before it harvests.
  - A **legacy replay** of any observer is not settled: it restarts from a plan (section 3), which decision 0010 accepts as a redelivery in replay mode.
- **Legacy settlement.**
  1. **Allowlist.** The change's enumerations record every legacy execution of the key as an inventory row of kind legacy. The final enumeration seals the allowlist; admission refuses every other legacy job from then on (section 6), so the set is finite.
  2. **Boundary.** Every allowlisted execution is drained (section 2). Then the observer reads the committed tail from storage on the primary and stores it as the boundary `B`. Nothing of the executions runs between the drain and that read, so every position any of their steps has read lies at or below `B`. Each execution is then resumed, when the observer is not quarantined, under admission's allowlist rule; its steps start again under the step-start check and read no further than `B`. Bounding the reads adds no gap to a handover, because every earlier read lies at or below `B`, and it makes every execution finite however fast a partition grows.
  3. **Transitions.** Each execution ends with a terminal row (section 3), which the change applies before it commits:

     | Execution | Settles as |
     | --- | --- |
     | completes successfully | its handover is applied as `HandOverCaughtUpJob` (`Observer.Catchup.cs:159-191`), `PartitionCaughtUp` (`:136-147`) or the recovery callbacks apply it, without their transitions. Partitions it completed under the previous release are covered by it, so nothing is delivered again |
     | completes with failures | the same, with the partial handover today's call applies; its failed partitions are already failed entries, because `PartitionFailed` applies during a change |
     | a step meets an event no connected target declares | the partition becomes a hold at that event (decision 0010, section 5), which ends the step; the execution then ends as one of the other rows |
     | fails (`Failed`, its state kept) | no handover; accounted (step 4) |
     | the observer is quarantined | not resumed; accounted |
     | disappears without a terminal row (an operator removed it) | accounted, with none of its steps left |
     | does not end (a step that cannot drain or answer) | `DefinitionChangeTimeout` alerts; an operator decision ends it (step 5) |

  4. **Accounting.** An execution that did not complete is accounted step by step: a step is accounted when it is still in Orleans step storage, whose checkpoint the harvest takes, or has a Chronicle progress record. A step with neither succeeded under the previous release and was deleted (*Orleans 1.21* `Job.cs:62`); which partition it covered is not recorded anywhere. The job's stored `TotalSteps` minus its accounted steps is its unaccounted count; an unreadable or inconsistent count counts as unaccounted. An execution with none is settled like any drained job.
  5. **Decision.** Unaccounted steps are coverage that cannot be reconstructed. They are surfaced in the Workbench, the CLI and a warning alert naming the observer, the namespace, the executions and their counts, and the change waits. It goes on in one of two ways: the execution becomes runnable again (the quarantine is cleared) and the settlement resumes it to `B`, or an operator runs **allow legacy redelivery** for the observer in one namespace or all of them, authorized, with a required reason and an audit-only system event, as forced completion. That settles the execution as drained: partitions it does not account for are caught up again from `Next`, or from their failed-partition position, so their prefix is delivered again, once. The decision applies to that settlement only and is published with the commit.
  6. **After the commit** the settlement is recorded as done in the control record, its rows are removed, and legacy jobs are refused for good.
- **Delivery.** Slices land on `main` dark: schema additions are additive and nullable, the only new write is the namespace baseline, and new paths stay unreachable until the gate. The order: (1) decision 0010's reader scopes, which keep the legacy reading of every request; (2) state and storage for both records, including the progress-record, row, inventory and terminal stores, the compatibility contract, the `ChangingDefinition` and `WaitingForHandler` members, the namespace baseline write and the downgrade refusal below; (3) contracts, released as a minor so Cratis/cli builds against published packages, with their operations answering `ClusterUpgradeInProgress`; (4) decision 0010's routing, holds and retirement; (5) this record's engine on Cratis.Orleans 1.21, with admission registered and passing every uninitialized or out-of-scope observer through; (6) Workbench, the CLI release, the .NET client and documentation; (7) the gate, last, which also enables initialization from the baseline, the bootstrap decision, legacy failed entries and the legacy settlement. The routing and engine slices are safe on their own only because nothing reaches them before the gate.
- **The downgrade floor** is the gate release, the first that writes the control record; the release note states it and names the minimum CLI version. A silo of any release from the state slice on that finds an initialized control record refuses to activate the observer and logs that the kernel is below the floor. Releases before the state slice cannot be changed: an older silo rewriting a failed partition drops hold fields, and the new enum members are unknown to it. An older silo joining after initialization is a downgrade below the floor and is unsupported.
- **During the rollout** nothing is worse than today, and the only new state, the namespace baseline, is ignored by older silos. A rollback below the state slice leaves baselines that its silos do not update; rolling forward again rewrites each one at that namespace observer's next subscription, and one not rewritten before the gate opens can initialize a namespace with the set it had before the rollback. Not guaranteed until the gate opens: no loss across a type change, the #4987 fix, and recovery of events skipped while the rollout lasts; those losses stay undetectable, as in decision 0004 Q7. Once the gate is open, rolling client versions are covered in any order.

## Options considered

- **Readers that run across the change with a revision cutover.** Not provable against the in-memory tail and callback side effects. Rejected.
- **Job status as the drain barrier.** Orleans persists `Stopped` before the steps stop. Rejected for `StopAndDrain`.
- **Awaiting a drain inside the observer's turn.** Step work and job hooks call the non-reentrant observer, so the two block each other. Rejected for polling with `TimeSpan.Zero` from a timer.
- **Draining through `IJobs.StopAndDrain` and taking `NotFound` as gone.** Its `NotFound` comes from job storage, which does not know a job whose `Start` passed admission but has not written yet. Rejected for draining through the job grain by the identity registered before `Start`.
- **An atomic admission reservation in Cratis.Orleans.** Would make admission and the first write one step. Not needed: a non-reentrant job grain already orders a drain after a `Start` in its turn, and a `Start` after the drain asks admission again. Not requested.
- **One non-interleaved commit turn as the guard against late callbacks.** `[AlwaysInterleave]` callbacks run at any await of it. Rejected for no-ops during the change, the synchronous latch and re-checks after every await.
- **A progress record per handled batch.** About a hundred times the step writes of every reactor and webhook job, permanently, for a rare event. Rejected for records at durable checkpoints and outcomes.
- **Retaining succeeded step outcomes in Cratis.Orleans** (opt-in retention with a step-outcomes query). Would make progress records unnecessary, but is not in 1.21. Deferred to an Orleans issue if the records prove costly.
- **Keeping replacement jobs after completion until the observer acknowledges them** (`KeepAfterCompleted`). Works for replays only and leaves the retention to each job type. Rejected for a terminal row written by every job of an initialized observer, which admission checks at `Start`.
- **Continuations, or the job list, inside the control record.** One document and one turn grow with the partition or job count. Rejected for rows.
- **Dropping unconsumed continuations and plans at the next change.** Their work would restart from the older global `Next` or vanish. Rejected for carrying them.
- **Deferring a quarantined observer's change until the quarantine ends.** The window below the fence becomes unbounded and silent. Rejected, except where a legacy settlement would otherwise redeliver or run inherited work while quarantined, and then the wait is alerted and an operator can end it.
- **Initialization as a quiesce.** Every observer would wait out its timeout at the end of the rollout, all at once. Rejected.
- **Initializing from the shared definition.** Last writer among namespaces; it can install a type a namespace never handled without a fence. Rejected for the namespace baseline.
- **Waiting for legacy jobs until they complete.** Unbounded on a busy partition, undefined for failed, held, quarantined or vanished jobs, and blind to partitions completed before the upgrade when the job does not complete. Rejected for the legacy settlement.
- **Draining legacy jobs of reactors and accepting the redelivery.** Repeats side effects of partitions completed before the upgrade without anyone choosing to. Rejected; redelivery happens only through allow legacy redelivery or forced completion.
- **Enabling the quiesce on Chronicle's own resume guards before Orleans admission.** Covers only the resume paths that reach `CanResume` or Chronicle's code. Rejected.
- **A bridge minor with an activation marker.** Enforces the floor in software but adds a release. Rejected for a documented floor; the dark slices give a downgrade refusal without an extra release.
- **Fencing abandoned subscriber calls.** Would hold every retry, not only a change, until the client answers or disconnects: delivery fencing for the whole engine. Left to #5014 and decision 0004.

## Default if unanswered

Decision 0010 cannot be enabled, so events of an added type keep being lost silently whenever versions alternate and clients with different sets keep replacing each other. Enabling 0010 on a weaker mechanism would let a reader cross the change and skip what this record guarantees it cannot.

## Timeline and scope

The record ships with decision 0010 in the delivery order of section 9, and its engine slice cannot merge before Chronicle takes a Cratis.Orleans 1.21 release. Every new-behavior specification is red before its change.

1. **State and storage** (in slice 2): the intent fields, `ChangingDefinition`, the progress-record store, the continuation, restart-plan and inventory rows, terminal rows, the settlement state, the namespace baseline and its write on every subscription, and the downgrade refusal, on every provider. *Verify:* round trips on InMemory, MongoDB and SQL; the baseline survives a save by the previous release on MongoDB and SQL; the refusal spec in [Verification](#verification).
2. **Contracts** (slice 3): complete definition change, allow legacy redelivery, initialize observer, and the definition-change and settlement state in the observer's read model.
3. **Engine** (slice 5): entering a change, the inventory and its registration before `Start`, drain through the grain, progress records, terminal rows, harvest, carried rows, commit and resume, revisions on jobs, callbacks and batches, the callback rules of section 5, Chronicle's `MayRun` with its pass-through and the legacy step-start check, forced completion with its reason and audit event, metrics and alerts. *Verify:* Core.Specs and integration specs in `Integration/Client/for_Observers/` and `Integration/Clustering/`.
4. **Gate** (slice 7): the marker grain type with admission, the gate check, `HoldTypeSetGate`, initialization from the baseline, the bootstrap decision, legacy failed entries and the legacy settlement. *Verify:* the gate, initialization and settlement specs.

**Out of scope:** fencing abandoned subscriber calls (#5014); retaining succeeded step outcomes in Cratis.Orleans; an automated rollout with an older Chronicle binary in the same cluster; recovering, for a reactor, which partitions a legacy step completed before the upgrade.

## Open questions

None of the design is open. One dependency is outside this repository: section 2 relies on job grains not being reentrant and on `IJob` and `IJobDrain` having no interleaving methods, which holds in Cratis.Orleans 1.21 but is pinned there only by the absence of attributes. Cratis.Orleans is asked to pin it by specification; if it ever changes, draining through the grain stops being proof and this record has to be revisited.

Answered:

- **A membership change restarts a running replay.** The replayed prefix is delivered again in replay mode; deferring the change until the replay ends would instead exclude events of the added type appended meanwhile. Restart plans keep their finite bounds, are started at most once (section 4), keep catch-up out of what they own until they are consumed, and a held or failed replay clears no fence (decision 0010, section 5).
- **Forced completion** uses the existing authorization with a required reason and an audit event shown in the Workbench; operator roles are Cratis/Chronicle#5015 (section 8).
- **The downgrade refusal** from the state slice on is taken: the gate release is the downgrade floor, stated in its release note; there is no bridge release (section 9).
- **`HoldTypeSetGate`** ships as a rollout-only setting, off by default and documented with when to use it (section 9).
- **The first change after the upgrade** redelivers once only for replay-safe observers; reactors, webhooks and event-store subscriptions go through the legacy settlement, which redelivers only on an operator's decision (section 9).
- **The subscriber-timeout overlap** is accepted because it is pre-existing and this decision does not widen it; it is tracked in Cratis/Chronicle#5014 (section 8).
- **The quiesce waits for Orleans owner admission:** it stays disabled until Cratis.Orleans 1.21 ships, every silo runs 1.21 or later, and every silo registers `AddJobAdmission` (section 9).

## Verification

**Done when:**

- no reader of an older revision runs or advances progress after a commit, and no job of the key runs while a change is pending except an allowlisted legacy execution under settlement, which reads no further than `B` and has ended before `J` is read;
- registration transitions add no duplicate delivery, except through forced completion, allow legacy redelivery and the replay-safe one-time redelivery of section 9; at-least-once redelivery after a crash or timeout is unchanged, because subscriber effects precede the durable outcome (`Observer.Handling.cs:255, 331-357`); a delivery abandoned by the subscriber timeout may overlap its partition's retry, as today (#5014);
- every change ends, through its own progress, a visible operator decision, or forced completion, without editing storage;
- while any silo lacks the marker, behavior matches today and nothing new is written but the namespace baseline.

**Verify by** these specifications, each red before its change:

- **Drain barrier:** a step still writing its checkpoint when its job is persisted `Stopped` is not harvested until `StopAndDrain` answers `Drained`; `TimedOut` is polled again on a later tick; the observer never awaits a drain inside its turn; a step's subscriber wait finishes before it stops.
- **In-flight start:** a revision-*r* `Start` admitted before the intent, whose first write stalls past both enumerations and past the observer's call timeout, is drained through its grain after its turn, and no step of it delivers after the commit; the same `Start` arriving after a drain that answered `Drained` for a never-started grain is refused; an `IJobs.StopAndDrain` answer of `NotFound` is never taken as drained. A job started before initialization whose write lands after the intent has every step stop at its start.
- **Many jobs:** a key with 10,000 failed partitions, each with a running retry job, drains with at most 100 drain calls and 1,000 row operations per tick, its control record does not grow with the job count, and registrations are handled between ticks.
- **Interleaving callbacks:** a `CaughtUp` suspended at its state write when a change begins, resuming after the commit, neither enters `Routing` nor clears revision *r + 1*'s markers; `RegisterCatchingUpPartitions` and the three recovery callbacks change nothing during a change; a revision-*r* call resuming at any await of the commit turn changes nothing.
- **Stale results:** a `CaughtUp`, retry completion, `ReportHandledEvents`, `PartitionFailed` or queue batch of an older revision arriving after the commit changes nothing, and its loss leaves no partition repeated; a completion call whose terminal row was already applied changes nothing; one that never arrives is applied from its row by the watchdog.
- **Harvest:** a catch-up stopped mid-partition continues from its last durable position; a partition whose step succeeded, with its step state deleted and `OnStepCompletedOrStopped` never run, is not repeated; the same for a retry; a job that completed just before the change, whose `CaughtUp` became a no-op, loses nothing; a step that handled 70 and failed at 71 resumes from its entry at 71 with no continuation; a failed terminal-record write closes the step instead of reporting success; a failing terminal-row write leaves the job `Failed` with its state; a step writes one record per durable checkpoint and one per outcome.
- **Carried obligations:** commit *r + 1* with P's continuation at 70, crash before its job starts, then a widening registration after reactivation: *r + 2* continues P from 70, not from `Next`; the same for an unstarted full-replay plan and a partition-replay plan, whose remaining types are reconciled with the new members and which clears without a job when none remain; rows of the pending change survive a crash and the sweep.
- **Large key:** a harvest of 100,000 partitions spreads over ticks, the control record's size does not depend on the partition count, and registrations are handled between ticks.
- **Join position:** an `E` event committed to storage but not yet in the grain's tail lies below `J`; an append in flight during the tail read is delivered; an empty sequence fences at `First`; the tail is read from the primary.
- **Missed events:** `E` events appended between `J0` and `J` raise the alert with their count.
- **Quarantine:** a change while quarantined drains, commits with its fences at that commit's `J`, stays quarantined, and resumes when the quarantine is cleared.
- **Watchdog:** during a change it neither unsubscribes, rescues jobs nor fast-forwards; it alerts.
- **Admission,** against Cratis.Orleans 1.21: an old-revision job is refused on explicit `Resume`, on rehydration while `Running` (stopped with a pending drain, no step work), and at a stage advance; a job of the current revision is refused while a change is pending; a job with a terminal row is refused at `Start`; a legacy job not on the allowlist is refused while a change is pending and after the first commit; a throwing `MayRun` leaves the job unchanged and not running; the observer never awaits a refusal in its turn; with admission registered and no control record initialized, every job is admitted and behavior matches today.
- **Replay restart:** a change during replay restarts it with the new set; a bounded, selected-type partition replay restarts with its bounds and remaining types; a crash after the commit, before or after the replacement job is stored, starts it exactly once; a replacement that completes, and is deleted by Orleans, before its plan row is removed is not started again, including an empty replay that completes inside its `Start`; a failed replacement clears no fence.
- **Bounded and recoverable:** a subscriber that never answers stops being waited for at the subscriber timeout and its partition fails; a job that never drains raises the alert after `DefinitionChangeTimeout`; forced completion is refused without a reason, and with one appends its audit event and commits; a silo crash before, during and after the commit converges, including a crash mid-drain, which Orleans completes on reactivation without resuming the job.
- **Gate:** `AddChronicleToSilo` never registers the marker without admission; `HoldTypeSetGate` keeps the gate closed; with an older silo present, or a silo whose manifest is missing, behavior matches today (including the #4987 hand-over) and nothing new is written but the baseline; after it leaves, observers initialize on their next registration. `ClusteringFixture` runs one binary in process (`Integration/Clustering/ClusteringFixture.cs:22-33`), so an older silo is simulated by a silo without the marker and admission; a rollout with a real older binary is not covered by these specifications.
- **Initialization:** observers re-registering at the end of the rollout do not enter `ChangingDefinition`; a legacy catch-up paused by the reconnect resumes and completes as revision 1; namespace X writes `[A, E]` to the shared definition while namespace Y's baseline is `[A]`: Y initializes with members `[A]`, and `E` joins Y through a change with a fence; a registration whose filters differ from the baseline is rejected; without a baseline an idle observer initializes with the registration's types, and one with a backlog stays uninitialized, alerting, until initialize observer; a legacy failed entry retries in live mode from its last attempt and gets its owner at its first recovery job.
- **Legacy settlement,** for a reactor, a webhook and an event-store subscription: partition P completed under the previous release and Q is unfinished when the first change begins; the settlement drains, resumes Q to `B` and applies the job's handover: P is not delivered again; a partition appended to faster than it is handled still ends at `B`; a completion with failures, a step meeting a missing handler (a hold), no connected target (holds), a `Failed` job, a quarantined observer and a job removed by an operator each settle as section 9 states; with unaccounted steps the change waits and alerts, clearing the quarantine resumes it, and allow legacy redelivery redelivers only partitions the execution does not account for; forced completion records the same decision; a replay-safe observer's legacy job is drained and only its unaccounted partitions are delivered again.
- **Downgrade refusal:** a silo of a release from the state slice on that finds an initialized control record refuses to activate the observer. This specification passes or fails.
- **Sweep:** removes records of vanished jobs older than the response timeout, applies terminal rows whose call was lost, keeps rows of a pending change, and never runs during a change.

## Consequences

- **Easier:** decision 0010's changes are applied without a reader crossing them, and the argument rests on two engine guarantees with their own specifications rather than on timing; work started from a continuation or plan survives any number of later changes; the first change after the upgrade never repeats a reactor's side effects without someone choosing to.
- **Harder:** a change pauses that namespace's observer while it drains; the release waits for Cratis.Orleans 1.21; jobs, callbacks and queue batches carry revisions; every job start writes an inventory row first and every job end a terminal row; steps write progress records; the rollout completes only when every silo runs the gate release; the gate release is a downgrade floor; a reactor's, webhook's or event-store subscription's first change after the upgrade waits for its inherited jobs to reach `B`, and can wait for an operator; a namespace observer with a backlog and no baseline needs an operator to initialize it.
- **Ruled out:** readers that span a membership change; enabling the quiesce before every silo runs owner admission; taking storage `NotFound` as proof that a job cannot run; silent redelivery of a side-effecting observer's inherited work.
