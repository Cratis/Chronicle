# Give projections and reducers durable generations, publish one Active generation per materialization, and resolve reads explicitly

Proposed on 2026-10-20 for [Cratis/Chronicle#5157](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5157), in [epic #5155](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5155). Offered to woksin for a decision; not accepted or implemented. Number 0014 leaves 0013 to [PR #5097](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/pull/5097).

## Context

A definition deployment, a historical rebuild, a serving switch and a query's version selection are different operations. Without durable identities and one serving authority, definition history and applied state remain ambiguous, replay work can interfere with live work, and a reader cannot name the state it needs. This record fixes the vocabulary and boundaries before the implementation issues choose APIs.

The following facts describe `origin/main` at `ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef`, not the proposed generation model:

- Definitions are registered by projection identifier; MongoDB replaces the stored definition for that identifier. `Projection.SetDefinition` rejects older software versions, replaces its grain state for admitted registrations, and notifies on a structural difference or legacy-storage upgrade. The comparer calls `ProjectionDefinitionNormalization.ForComparison`, which nulls the `ReadModel` identifier (hence ignoring a read-model/sink retarget), `InitialModelState` and metadata `LastUpdated`, `Tags`, `Version`. `IsActive` is still compared; `ProjectionDefinition` has no `Sink` property ([storage](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.MongoDB/Projections/ProjectionDefinitionsStorage.cs#L46-L61), [registration](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Projections/Projection.cs#L60-L114), [comparison](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Projections/Engine/ProjectionDefinitionComparer.cs#L35-L45), [normalization](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Projections/Engine/ProjectionDefinitionNormalization.cs#L21-L43), [definition](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Concepts/Projections/Definitions/ProjectionDefinition.cs#L39-L86)). There is no retained generation/revision lineage in these paths.
- `ProjectionGeneration` already exists as `record ProjectionGeneration(uint Value) : ConceptAs<uint>(Value)`, with `FirstValue = 1U`, `First` and an `Unspecified` instance whose value is `uint.MaxValue`. It supplies value conversion, not a durable allocator or lifecycle. MongoDB saves its replacement definition under the `ProjectionGeneration.First` key ([concept](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Concepts/Projections/ProjectionGeneration.cs#L6-L32), [save](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.MongoDB/Projections/ProjectionDefinitionsStorage.cs#L46-L61)).
- Full replay takes the observer into replay rather than continuing its normal live route. Replay completion goes through `CompleteReplay`; that clears replay state and returns a non-quarantined observer to routing. Generation rebuilds must not confuse that job lifecycle with publication of a new served definition ([routing](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/States/Routing.cs#L151-L170), [completion](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/Jobs/ReplayObserver.cs#L325-L361), [resume](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/Observer.Replay.cs#L75-L152)).
- MongoDB's sink selects normal or replay collections through `_isReplaying`; after claiming replay under a temporary name, publication moves served to a revert name and claimed replay to served in two separate commands. SQL uses a transaction around its rename swap. These are replay-container mechanisms, not an atomic multi-generation serving contract ([MongoDB](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.MongoDB/Sinks/SinkCollections.cs#L45-L146), [SQL](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.Sql/Sinks/Sink.ReplayPromotion.cs#L22-L65)).
- `ReplayContext` holds `Type` (`ReadModelType`), `ContainerName` and `RevertContainerName` (`ReadModelContainerName`), and `Started` (`DateTimeOffset`). `ReadModelOccurrence` holds `ObserverId` (`ObserverId`), `Occurred` (`DateTimeOffset`), `Type` (`ReadModelType`), and the same two container-name fields. `ReadModelType` contains an identifier and a read-model generation, not an archived schema. Neither record stores a proven applied event cursor, so neither establishes that a retained snapshot is caught up ([context record](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage/ReadModels/ReplayContext.cs#L10-L24), [occurrence record](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage/ReadModels/ReadModelOccurrence.cs#L10-L22), [read-model type](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Concepts/ReadModels/ReadModelType.cs#L6-L11)).
- `MaterializedReadModelStore` receives a `ReadModelDefinition`; its `GetSinkFor` calls the namespace's `Sinks.GetFor(definition)` without a projection-generation selector ([query resolution](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/ReadModels/MaterializedReadModelStore.cs#L37-L64), [sink resolution](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/ReadModels/MaterializedReadModelStore.cs#L88-L89)). Global projections route their materialization to the `NotSet` namespace's sink, while observer keys include the source namespace and sequence ([pipeline](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Projections/Engine/Pipelines/ProjectionPipelineManager.cs#L69-L98), [observer key](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Concepts/Observation/ObserverKey.cs#L9-L19)).
- Reducers replace registered definition state, subscribe through a connected client and evolve definitions through replay plans. They need generation-specific client execution as well as separate storage ([registration](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/Reducers/Clients/Reducer.cs#L57-L112), [evolution](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/Reducers/Clients/Reducer.cs#L146-L207)). The existing evolution policy is `Automatic`, `PartialOnly` or `Manual`; this proposal retains those names ([policy](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/DefinitionEvolutionPolicy.cs#L8-L24)).

## Decision

Behind a default-off kernel feature flag, give each logical projection or reducer an immutable generation lineage and a separate immutable definition-revision history; build new generations in isolated containers while the current generation remains live, publish exactly one Active generation through the sink's single serving authority under an Orleans-owned fenced handover, retain displaced generations according to their actual following/snapshot behavior, and make ordinary reads follow Active while explicit versioned reads resolve and pin a named compatible generation. The numbered clauses below define this proposed contract; none describes an already available API.

### 1. Identity, revisions and materialization scope

A logical identity is `(EventStore, ProjectionId)` or `(EventStore, ReducerId)` with an explicit kind; it is not an application software version or a read-model schema generation. Reuse the existing `ProjectionGeneration` concept rather than replace it; extend its use with durable monotonic allocation per logical identity and the lifecycle/storage contract below. Allocate from `First` through `uint.MaxValue - 1`, reserve zero and `Unspecified`, never reuse a number, and fail allocation on exhaustion. Allocate immutable `DefinitionRevision` values within a generation. A generation's materialization identity adds the namespace for namespaced models, or the event-store-wide global scope for a shared model. Namespace-local states and cursors may differ even when the definition and generation number are shared.

Keep a canonical fingerprint and full definition snapshot for **every** revision, not just the current one. Canonicalize mapping, event-type capabilities, filters, keys, joins, children, scope, `InitialModelState`, the target `ReadModel` identifier and its resolved sink configuration; ordering without semantics is normalized. Reducers must declare an implementation revision/fingerprint: the kernel cannot infer a delegate's code changes. Deliberately separate operational activation flags (including `IsActive`, which the current comparer compares), connection IDs and promotion policies from semantic fingerprints. Explicitly classify read-model/sink retargets and initial-state changes even when the current comparer returns Same; a materialization-destination change requires a new isolated generation. Scope/key changes also require classification, not an assumption that the current comparer ignores them. Store fingerprint algorithm/version and verify full canonical equality on a hash match. No historical fingerprint is overwritten.

With generations enabled, an admitted `FullReplay` definition evolution, a semantics-changing reducer implementation or an explicit generation rebuild creates a new generation with empty isolated storage, even when rebuilding the same revision to repair data. Admitted `NoAction` creates a new revision in the same lineage. Proven-safe `PartialReplay` creates a pending immutable revision in the same lineage and records per-partition adoption receipts; no served revision is advertised as fully applied before its proof completes. Unsafe/unknown evolution requires a full generation. [#5160](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5160) owns shadow publication. Until that proof exists, definition changes classified for partial replay take the full-replay path, not partial replay into the live read model. The bounded patch in [#5171](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5171) ships independently now; safe generation evolution supersedes that fallback later, not before its proof.

A registration matching any historical fingerprint reuses its immutable definition snapshot. It neither restores it nor cancels a candidate. The same fingerprint may occur in several repair generations; registration does not choose a materialization, and version-read bindings must identify one generation/revision explicitly or report ambiguity. An explicit desired-definition change, not an old replica's reconnect, supersedes a candidate; a deliberate return to the active fingerprint abandons that candidate without creating another generation. Keep desired, applied and per-materialization served revisions distinct.

### 2. Generation and observer identity

Import existing served state as generation 1 and keep its existing `ObserverId = ProjectionId` (or reducer ID). Later generations use a collision-checked generated ID such as `<id>@g<n>`, allocated with a durable unique binding to `(logical identity, generation, materialization scope)`. Reserve names atomically; fail on an existing unrelated owner, never parse an arbitrary user ID's suffix as a binding. Promotion and retirement do not rename observer state. Definitions, subscriber activation/deactivation, pipeline caches, bulk/replay handlers, futures, notifications and client dispatch all resolve the binding and explicit revision. They never interpret a generated observer ID as a new logical projection ID or fall back to a silo's latest cached definition.

Each generation has independent positions, failed partitions, jobs, locks and explicitly addressed sinks. Generation building does not flip a shared sink-wide replay flag. Join/child lookups stay inside the same generation. `Building` historical delivery uses replay observation semantics; live delivery in `Following`, `Active` and `Retired` does not. Generation lifecycle is separate from observer running state and handover phase.

### 3. Lifecycle

These are the **only** generation lifecycle states:

| State | Meaning | Allowed next states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building | Allocated, possibly waiting for admission, rebuilding history into isolated storage; never default-served. | Following, Abandoned |
| Following | Historical coverage is proven; follows live events before publication. | Active, Abandoned |
| Active | The one generation selected by the serving authority; follows live events. | Retired |
| Retired | Previously served, still follows live events and remains a bounded rollback target. | Active, Archived |
| Archived | Non-following snapshot with its last proven cursor vector, or explicitly unknown legacy position. | Removed |
| Abandoned | Unserved canceled, superseded or terminally failed build; drained before cleanup. | Removed |
| Removed | Containers are gone; immutable audit/identity metadata remains. | None |

Creation enters `Building`; migration alone imports the existing served generation as `Active`. Import old revert containers as `Archived`, never `Retired`. Retriable build failure stays `Building` with failure metadata. Queued/admitted, fenced, draining, promotion failed and waiting for a compatible client are operational metadata, not extra lifecycle states. Retrying an `Abandoned` or `Removed` generation allocates a new number.

Promotion pairs `Following → Active` with the previous `Active → Retired`; rollback pairs `Retired → Active` with `Active → Retired` through the same protocol. A zero rollback window immediately schedules `Retired → Archived`, not a fictional following snapshot. Removal always drains/archives or abandons first and honors read pins; no direct deletion of Active. An Archived generation cannot instantly roll back: rebuild a new generation or explicitly catch up into a new lineage before publication.

### 4. Storage names and one serving authority per sink

Persist a container binding per `(logical identity, materialization scope, sink, generation)`. Adopt the existing MongoDB collection or SQL table `c` as generation 1 **in place**, with its current data, indexes and name: no rename, collection-to-view conversion, copy or maintenance window. New generations use `c-g<n>` before provider-safe encoding; new installations use that format from generation 1. Persist each final physical name once and never rename it on promotion or rollback. A bounded readable prefix plus a hash of the complete owner tuple leaves room for the generation suffix; apply the provider's identifier/namespace length and equality rules, quote SQL identifiers, reserve names, and fail on collisions rather than silently truncate or adopt another owner's data. Multiple logical producers of one logical container need an explicit shared materialization owner or fail admission. Database placement does not change. Unsupported/custom sinks reject generation capability before allocating or mutating a build; the flag-off legacy path remains supported.

**One binding authority on both sink families:** a durable Active binding contains the generation, immutable definition revision/schema, physical destination, serving epoch and handover receipt. Conditional publication checks the expected epoch and old generation and changes that binding atomically; the binding commit is the linearization point. Lifecycle/catalog copies are derived and reconciled, never independently committed serving pointers. MongoDB uses one authoritative binding document with conditional replacement; SQL uses a transactional binding row and receipt. Neither requires promotion DDL. A lost response rereads the authority and receipt rather than guessing. Writers and pinned reads always address immutable physical destinations; readers resolve the Active binding once per operation under a lease. Following a binding must not depend on a stale per-silo cache.

Direct database readers of `c` retain the original collection/table and its operations; after an opt-in promotion, that name still identifies generation 1, **not** an alias for Active. Opting in therefore requires applications that need to follow Active to use binding-aware Chronicle reads or an explicitly coordinated binding-aware adapter. Never silently convert existing direct readers or promise a stable-name automatic cutover. With the flag off their behavior is unchanged. MongoDB view operator/index-hint compatibility and view change-stream restrictions largely dissolve because no view is introduced; query compatibility of binding-aware reads and physical change-stream rebinding still need integration proof. This is an application opt-in requirement, not a forced data migration or maintenance window.

Only one sink is authoritative for a served materialization. Cross-database or multi-sink atomic promotion is not promised. All providers, including in-memory storage, must preserve these observable semantics or explicitly reject the capability.

### 5. Orleans ownership, fence and recovery

The `Projection` grain, whose [existing key is projection ID plus event store](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Concepts/Projections/ProjectionKey.cs#L8-L19), has the **proposed extended role** of immutable definition/generation catalog and monotonic allocator; reducers receive an equivalent kind-qualified catalog. A new non-reentrant `IProjectionHandover` grain, keyed by `(EventStore, logical identity, materialization scope)`, owns durable materialization-generation state, container bindings, admission epoch and the promotion/rollback journal. For global scope there is exactly one event-store-wide owner, not one promotion owner per contributing namespace. It alone authorizes publication and retention; per-silo replay finalizers cannot promote independently. Large partition inventories live in revisioned storage rows owned by it, not one unbounded grain-state document.

Orleans single activation and serialized turns give one coordinator for the scope and serialize competing build, promote, rollback and retention requests. **They do not by themselves drain jobs, flush silo-local buffers, make sink DDL transactional or stop delayed external writes.** Persist handover ID, owner epoch, source/target, phase, deadline, inventory revision and cursor vector before issuing work. Close observer/job admission; poll admitted job grains with non-blocking `StopAndDrain(TimeSpan.Zero)` on later turns. Only `Drained`/`Completed` proves that barrier; timeout, missing storage or a stopped status does not. Never await a positive drain timeout in the observer turn its jobs call back into. This follows [decision 0011's drain/admission contract](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/decisions/0011-a-type-set-change-quiesces-the-observer-through-orleans-drain-and-admission.md#L77-L82).

After draining all admitted deliveries, jobs and callbacks and flushing every contributing writer, capture committed/scanned `N[K]`, where `K = (EventStore, Namespace, EventSequenceId)`. Require the incoming generation to prove coverage through every component, not merely a small numeric lag or a job's completion. Preserve failed/unfinished partition obligations below the shared cursor. Global publication freezes contributor admission, persists the complete contributor set and namespace-membership revision, and rechecks it at commit. Never compare namespace-local sequence numbers or take their maximum. [#5172](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5172) must first establish safe global-scope admission; uncoordinated namespace replay of a shared materialization remains rejected.

The fence consists of closed admission, reconciled obligations, drained/flushed writers and sink-enforced owner/write epochs. Reject stale callbacks and stale writers at commit; single activation is not a substitute. Immutable physical destinations on both sink families avoid redirecting writes during publication. Fence all affected writers, persist their own committed vectors and acknowledge the new epoch before release; legitimate Retired writers may resume only under that generation's fresh admission. An unreachable writer blocks/aborts before commit; it is not presumed drained. Resume each generation above its own proven committed/scanned position, not a copied job checkpoint.

Persist phases `Fencing → Draining → Swapping → Swapped → Releasing → Completed`, with pre-commit `Aborting`. After a crash or unknown acknowledgment, read the sink authority/receipt: before commit an expired attempt may abort after excluding delayed publication; after commit finish forward, reconcile lifecycle/served-definition metadata and release. Unknown authority leaves fences in place for recovery, never guesses rollback. Publication commands must be serialized and reconciled before any owner epoch is transferred; sink-side epoch admission must reject a delayed old-owner binding publish, write, create/drop or cleanup command. Binding compare-and-swap does not fence a data write or DDL command by itself. The sink protocol must prove stale-operation exclusion before owner transfer; otherwise leave the fence closed and generation enablement refused. Notifications are idempotent hints backed by authority/epoch rechecks.

### 6. Reads and notifications

An ordinary query reads **Active**, not newest registered, newest schema, newest software version, Building or Following. Resolve one serving epoch for the whole request (including joins/paging); concurrent publication can return the old complete generation or the new complete generation, never a mixture or empty interval. MongoDB and SQL reads use the authoritative Active binding with coordinated leases/epoch checks, not a potentially stale cached `ActiveGeneration`. Code requiring multi-request consistency uses an explicit generation pin.

[#5163](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5163) owns the explicit versioned-read API: a selector resolves a software-version registration to one `(generation, definition revision, schema)` with proven coverage, then obtains a retention pin. Software version alone is not generation identity and a raw physical name is not a selector. Explicit generation reads may inspect Following, Retired or Archived data with lifecycle/position metadata and an opt-in for unserved state. Building is diagnostic-only with an explicit incomplete-state acknowledgment. Removed, incompatible, unready or ambiguous selections fail with an actionable error; never fall back silently to Active. Pins are bounded, renewable leases that block deletion; operation pins do not require an indefinitely held database transaction. Version-pinned reads remain on that generation after promotion/rollback, and Archived reads promise a snapshot, not freshness.

Observable queries watch only their resolved generation. Publication broadcasts `GenerationActivated` with serving epoch, invalidates read/pipeline caches, rebinds physical change streams and sends one full reload/reset to default delta subscribers without an intermediate empty payload. Pinned subscribers stay pinned. Reconnect and missed-broadcast handling re-read authority; a binding update is not assumed to deliver physical collection-change notifications.

### 7. Policy, retention, prerequisites and reducers

Keep `Automatic`, `PartialOnly` and `Manual` evolution policy. Full rebuild admission follows that policy: automatic starts a separate build; PartialOnly/Manual records the desired immutable candidate and recommendation without starting a full job. A recorded candidate may be `Building` with `BuildStarted = false`. The default stays **`Automatic`**: admitted rebuilds promote automatically once Following and the handover readiness proof are satisfied, without an approval command. Promotion gating is a separate, explicit opt-in for operator/deployment-condition/scheduled approval, for application or kernel-owned models; it must not silently change the default or the meaning of `Manual` build admission. [#5161](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5161) owns conditions, audit and policy precedence. Stale registrations never grant promotion or cancellation authority.

Default retention is one following Retired generation for 24 hours and one Archived snapshot; keep `ReplayedVersionsToKeep` as the snapshot-count compatibility alias. Kernel-owned models retain neither by default. [#5162](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5162) owns configuration and scheduling. Rollback is the same fenced publication with a Retired target and proven coverage; force may waive failed-partition policy, never coverage, drain, epoch or membership barriers. Each successful displacement gets a fresh rollback deadline; failed attempts do not extend it. Expiry/cap enforcement drains before archiving; pins defer removal. Cancellation/supersession drains a build into Abandoned before cleanup. Keep audit tombstones, definitions and identity bindings after physical removal.

Reducers are in scope for the same lineage, lifecycle, isolated sinks, reads and promotion guarantees. Rebuilding a reducer needs a declared implementation revision, an empty initial rebuild state and generation-aware client dispatch for both old and new implementations. No connected compatible target means waiting, not delivery to another version. [#5165](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5165) owns exact client-version selection/admission and must explicitly cover reducers; reactor-only version routing is insufficient. [#5166](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5166) owns ordered rebuild execution/parallelism, [#5167](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5167) worker placement, and [#5168](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5168) coverage/readiness/progress visibility. A position-distance estimate is never a publication proof.

For reactors, the related guarantee is **single delivery ownership and stable delivery identity across a reactor version change; external effects still need ReactorDelivery receipts/downstream idempotency**. [#5164](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5164) owns the handover and stable lineage identity, preserving unfinished obligations and at-least-once retries. Reactor version ownership is not dual delivery to following projection generations, and it does not guarantee exactly-once side effects.

### 8. Compatibility, release and initialization boundary

Projection generations ship as a **MINOR**, default-off, backward-compatible feature. Add a kernel `Features.ProjectionGenerations` flag defaulting to `false` (proposed name), following [`Features.ChangesetStorage`](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/Features.cs#L22-L25) and its [storage selection gate](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.MongoDB/EventStoreNamespaceStorage.cs#L94-L96). With the flag off, behavior is exactly today's supported legacy behavior: registration replacement/notifications, version ordering, containers, replay jobs, reads and evolution policy are unchanged by generations. Independent safety patches apply in both modes. No generation state adoption, routing, allocation or writes run merely because additive metadata support is installed. The existing [observer policy defaults to `Automatic`](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Configuration/Observers.cs#L80-L83); keep it, and automatic promotion after readiness when enabled. Approval/gated cutover is opt-in.

- **Minor:** opt-in immutable admission/history and generation-1 adoption; isolated Building/Following execution; fenced binding promotion, optional gates and rollback/retention; explicit versioned reads and additive commands/RPCs. Enabling requires compatible kernel capability and binding-aware readers; none becomes compulsory for existing installations.
- **Patch:** bounded correctness fixes remain independent of this feature, notably #5171: a definition change that would partially replay into the live read model instead uses full replay under the existing admission policy (otherwise recommends it). The current [automatic partial branch](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Projections/Projection.cs#L606-L646) schedules partition replay; [performing a replay recommendation](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/States/ReplayCandidateRecommendation.cs#L14-L17) already requests full replay. Do not remove explicit partition replay or claim this patch provides generations, uninterrupted live rebuilds or instant rollback.
- **Major, deferred to a future major:** default-on generations, mandatory approval, forced direct-reader routing/storage conversion, or changed legacy API/job-completion semantics. No default flips in the minor release series.

Preserve [`IProjections.Replay` signatures and `Task<JobId>`/`NotSet` contract](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Clients/DotNET/Projections/IProjections.cs#L137-L150) and the explicit [`ReplayPartition` command](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Observation/ReplayPartition.cs#L19-L32). They remain directed at Active (the legacy served model when disabled), with their existing job status/completion semantics; never reinterpret replay completion as pending approval or silently redirect a legacy replay to a candidate. Generation build/start/promote commands are separate, additive operations. No new abstract members may be added to user-implemented interfaces; provide separate optional capability interfaces/adapters instead. Reads without a selector remain on Active, regardless of caller software version; versioned reads are explicit opt-in. The current [key-read request fields 1–10](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Contracts/ReadModels/GetInstanceByKeyRequest.cs#L15-L70) have no version selector: preserve their numbers/meaning and RPC identities, add optional selectors with absent = Active, and reject unsupported explicit selection rather than silently fall back. Generate wire output through the repository workflow; require released-client compatibility and generated TypeScript, Kotlin, Elixir and Python client tests, not just a .NET round trip.

**Initial migration boundary:** install additive metadata/schema support first; then, only on explicit enablement for a compatible event store with legacy replay jobs completed/drained (or explicitly repaired), idempotently bind its current definition, observer state and existing physical container as generation 1 Active. No event or read-model data is copied, renamed or converted. Adoption preserves positions, failed partitions and IDs; incomplete legacy snapshots remain Archived with unknown proof, not fresh rollback targets. Use existing mechanisms: [`PatchManager` orders versioned candidates and checks patch history](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Patching/PatchManager.cs#L29-L59), then [calls `Up` before saving the receipt](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Patching/PatchManager.cs#L69-L86); [`ICanApplyPatch` exposes `Up`/`Down`](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Core/Patching/ICanApplyPatch.cs#L23-L33). Therefore adoption must be retry-safe across the Up/receipt crash window. A version-filtered startup patch alone cannot initialize an event store enabled later: an enablement initializer must perform/recheck binding adoption. SQL can use [`TableMigrator` for additive metadata tables](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.Sql/EventStores/Namespaces/TableMigrator.cs#L26-L66), not rename the read-model table. These are mechanisms to extend, not an already implemented generation migration or a cluster fence.

**Mixed-version fence:** no generation adoption or runtime enablement on a cluster containing kernels that cannot honor its admission/binding protocol. Existing [definition replacement](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.MongoDB/Projections/ProjectionDefinitionsStorage.cs#L46-L59) and [replay rename finalization](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/blob/ca0cb6237429f17971a5ba2a40b44061c31a00ef/Source/Kernel/Storage.MongoDB/Sinks/SinkCollections.cs#L98-L146) have no generation epoch check; a new coordinator cannot retrofit fencing into an old kernel. Keep the flag off during rolling upgrades; before enablement, #5169 must establish a durable compatibility/initialization marker, exclude old kernels and legacy jobs, drain outstanding commands and enforce sink-side write/DDL/cleanup epochs (section 5). Downgrading below that floor requires explicit deinitialization/recovery, not flipping the flag off after promotion. **Still open for the maintainer:** the enforceable old-kernel admission/downgrade-floor mechanism and provider-specific stale-write/DDL exclusion protocol. Do not infer either from PatchManager, Orleans single activation or a metadata CAS; without proof, refuse enablement. No collection-to-view compatibility or maintenance-window decision remains.

## Options considered

- **Isolated generation writers plus one fenced serving authority (chosen).** Keeps live serving independent of rebuild and makes versioned reads/rollback nameable, at the cost of additional storage and coordination.
- **One observer dual-writing old and new containers.** One position cannot describe independently rebuilt state or isolate failures; joins and replay/live ordering become ambiguous.
- **Latest software version or last registration wins.** A rolling deployment can replace definitions in either direction; a reconnect is not operator intent, and reducer execution could use the wrong code.
- **A new generation for every definition change.** Simple, but safe NoAction/partial evolution needlessly rebuilds history. Immutable revisions preserve its lineage without that cost.
- **Rename-only MongoDB serving.** Two renames expose a missing-name interval; replacing the target in one rename loses the displaced collection without a copy. Neither supplies the selected atomic visibility plus bounded rollback contract.
- **A MongoDB pointer document with a stable served view as another authority.** Two independent commits can disagree. Use the selected binding-only adoption instead: following Active is an explicit reader opt-in, and neither a view nor a plain container name overrides the binding. Existing direct readers are never silently redirected.
- **Collection-to-view conversion or SQL rename publication.** Rejected: forced conversion changes direct-reader semantics and needs a maintenance boundary; renaming also moves physical destinations under pinned readers/writers. Adopt existing containers in place and publish one binding instead. Direct readers opt in to binding resolution, never a forced alias conversion.
- **Archived snapshots as instant rollback.** Their cursor can be old or unknown. Retired means following; snapshots need a new catch-up/rebuild proof.

## Default if unanswered

This remains proposed and authorizes no generation implementation or release. Keep the existing supported behavior, but do not advertise uninterrupted live rebuild, atomic multi-generation publication or version-selected reads. Ship the conservative safety prerequisites [#5170](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5170), #5171 and #5172 on their own proof; do not enable unsafe partial/global paths while waiting. Delaying the decision blocks the dependent epic work and leaves deployment-time evolution operationally constrained.

## Timeline and scope

The decision holds for the generation feature in #5155 and its client/read/storage contracts. Import existing served state only after draining or explicitly repairing legacy replay sessions; preserve old observer IDs and schema history, label legacy snapshot positions unknown, and adopt existing containers through metadata/binding only, without a maintenance window. Keep the feature off unless compatibility and initialization are proven. Additive storage/contracts come before enabling new writes; do not migrate legacy jobs by guessing their generation. Protocol/schema changes follow the repository's generated-wire workflow, not hand-edited generated output.

[#5169](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5169) owns mixed-kernel-version rollout, capability negotiation and rollback floors. Application version coexistence does not prove kernel rollout safety. Gate generation enablement on compatible cluster capability; SQL multi-silo verification also depends on [#5127](https://github.com/Cratis/Chronicle/issues/5127). External side-effect transactions, distributed multi-sink atomicity and arbitrary cross-partition parallel projection execution are out of scope.

## Consequences

- Rebuild, serving and read-version choices become independent and auditable. Stable bindings and explicit revisions remove last-registration and per-silo-cache ambiguity.
- Following/Retired generations cost extra storage, writes and compatible reducer client capacity. Bounded retention and job placement become operational requirements, not optional polish.
- Publication adds durable orchestration, sink epochs, contributor vectors and recovery work. Stale write/DDL/cleanup exclusion and mixed-kernel admission remain blocking implementation proof, not assumed properties of Orleans serialization.
- Existing MongoDB collections and SQL tables remain generation 1 without conversion; direct readers keep that physical destination until they explicitly adopt binding-aware resolution. No maintenance window or forced reader migration is part of the minor release.
- The view-compatibility question is largely dissolved; the initial boundary is metadata/binding adoption after capability and legacy-job checks. Maintainer decisions remain on enforceable old-kernel exclusion/downgrade floors and provider fencing. Default-on generations or mandatory approval wait for a future major; this record remains proposed pending maintainer acceptance.
